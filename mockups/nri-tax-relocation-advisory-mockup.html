What is NRI & relocation advisory

Guidance for anyone moving into, or out of, India

Your Indian tax residency status, Non-Resident, Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), or Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR), determines whether India taxes only your Indian income, or your income worldwide. It's assessed fresh for every tax year, based on physical presence and a small number of special rules.

This service covers both directions: NRIs returning to India permanently and planning their RNOR window, and Indian residents relocating abroad who need to understand what happens to their India income, accounts, and disclosure obligations along the way.

At Cash Stream Advisors, we assess your residency position, plan the timing of your move where that's still possible, and handle the ongoing filing, whichever direction you're headed.

The RNOR window is usually available for about two years, and it's the single biggest planning opportunity most returning NRIs miss.
Highlight Box: Regulatory Callout
Income Tax Act 2025 Update The residency and RNOR rules that decide your status have not changed under the Income Tax Act, 2025, they remain at Section 6, unchanged in substance and in number. A few related provisions have been renumbered, effective 1 April 2026:
  • Sections 90 and 90A (DTAA agreements and adoption) are now Section 159
  • Section 91 (relief where no DTAA exists) is now Section 160, a separate section from 159
  • Form 15CA and Form 15CB (used for repatriating funds abroad) are now Form 145 and Form 146
References to the old numbers in earlier correspondence should be read accordingly.

Signature element: Residency Status Cards
The question that decides everything

Which Category Are You?

Your status for the year decides what India can tax, it isn't a single test, but most situations fall into one of three categories.

Non-Resident (NRI)

Under 182 Days in India

Generally applies if you're in India for fewer than 182 days in the tax year, and fewer than 60 days if you spent 365 days or more here across the prior four years. Only your Indian-source income is taxed.

RNOR

A Transitional Status

Often applies to returning NRIs, if you were a non-resident in 9 of the last 10 years, or in India for 729 days or fewer over the past 7 years. Foreign income generally stays outside Indian tax during this window.

Resident & Ordinarily Resident

Worldwide Income Taxed

Applies once you're a resident and don't qualify for RNOR, typically after settling back in India for a few years. Your global income becomes taxable in India from this point.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I an NRI, RNOR, or Resident this year?+
It depends on your physical presence in India during the tax year and your status in prior years. Under 182 days generally means Non-Resident. Returning NRIs often qualify for RNOR, a transitional status, for a limited window. We assess this based on your actual travel dates, not an estimate.
Do I need to declare my foreign salary or assets in my Indian tax return?+
Only if you're a Resident and Ordinarily Resident. Non-Residents and RNORs generally aren't required to disclose foreign assets or global income, only Indian-source income. Once you're a full Resident, foreign assets need to be disclosed in the FA schedule of your return.
Can I repatriate money from my India accounts abroad?+
Generally yes, subject to RBI rules and account type. NRO account funds have specific repatriation limits and documentation requirements, including a CA certificate on Form 145 and Form 146. NRE accounts are typically freely repatriable.
What happens to my India investments if I move abroad?+
Property, fixed deposits, and mutual funds in India remain yours and remain taxable in India on the income they generate, rent, interest, or capital gains, regardless of where you live. Your accounts typically need to be redesignated as NRO once your status changes.
How long can I keep RNOR status when I return to India?+
Typically around two years, depending on your specific history of days in India over prior years. The exact window depends on your individual facts, we calculate this precisely rather than assuming a standard length.
What's the difference between an NRE and an NRO account, and does it matter for tax?+
Yes, significantly. NRE accounts hold foreign earnings, are freely repatriable, and the interest is tax-exempt for NRIs. NRO accounts hold India-sourced income, have repatriation limits, and the interest is taxable. Mixing funds between the two causes real compliance problems.
I'm returning to India after many years abroad, how should I plan my move?+
Timing matters more than almost anything else. Moving a few months earlier or later can change your RNOR window by a full year. We review your travel history and recommend the optimal return date before you finalize your plans.
Do I need to file an Indian tax return if I have no income in India?+
If you have no India-sourced income and no India assets requiring compliance, a return may not be required. But if you retain property, bank accounts, or investments here, a return is usually still needed even at low income levels.
What is Schedule FA, and do I need to fill it out?+
Schedule FA is where Residents and Ordinarily Residents disclose foreign assets and income in their Indian tax return. It doesn't apply to Non-Residents or RNORs. Getting your residency status right is the first step in knowing whether this applies to you.
I'm moving abroad mid-year, how is my residency status determined?+
Your status is determined for the full tax year based on total days in India during that year, not the specific date you left. Depending on when you depart, you may still be a Resident for that entire year, with global income disclosure obligations, even after moving.
Can NRIs claim DTAA benefits on Indian income, and what documents do I need?+
Yes, where a treaty applies and the correct documentation is in place, typically a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41. This works alongside residency planning, we handle both together where relevant.
