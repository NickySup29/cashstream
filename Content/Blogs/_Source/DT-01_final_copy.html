What Is DTAA? How the Double Tax Avoidance Agreement Works in India

Reviewed by Cash Stream Advisors. Last reviewed: 22 November 2026.

The short answer

DTAA is the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. It is a treaty between two countries that decides which country may tax which income, and how the other country gives relief, so the same income is not taxed twice. It shares or limits taxing rights. It does not usually make income tax-free.

Most people meet the question when a payment is about to cross a border. A bank deducts tax on interest, a foreign client withholds tax on a fee, or a payer in India asks for papers before it will apply a lower rate. Behind each of these sits one idea. So, what is DTAA, and how does it work in India?

The short answer

DTAA is the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, a treaty between two countries. In plain terms, the double taxation avoidance agreement decides which country may tax which income, and how the other country gives relief, so that the same income is not taxed twice. It shares or limits taxing rights. It does not usually make income tax-free.

How a DTAA works: one example

Take an Indian resident who earns interest of 100,000 from a bank in another country. That country taxes it at the rate the treaty allows, and 10,000 is withheld there. India also taxes the interest in the resident's hands, and the Indian tax on it comes to 30,000. The treaty does not make the income tax-free. Instead, India gives credit for the 10,000 already paid, so the resident pays the remaining 20,000 in India.

The same logic works the other way. A foreign investor earning income in India is taxed here at the treaty-limited rate, and the investor's home country gives relief for it. The figures above are round numbers chosen to show the logic. They are not a treaty rate.

This is what people mean when they search for DTAA income tax: the income tax result of a cross-border payment, after the treaty has done its work.

The two relief methods: exemption and credit

Under the exemption method, the country of residence does not tax the foreign income at all. Under the credit method, it taxes the income but deducts the foreign tax already paid, up to the amount of its own tax on that income.

In the treaties our clients use most, India gives relief to its residents by credit, which the treaty text words as a deduction from Indian tax. This is so in the treaties with the US (Article 25), the UK (Article 24), the UAE (Article 25), Singapore (Article 25), Mauritius (Article 23) and Germany (Article 23). The UK and Germany articles are subject to Indian domestic law. The other country may relieve its own residents by exemption for some income. Germany, for example, uses exemption or credit depending on the income type. The treaty article for the income in question decides.

Section 90 Income Tax Act and Section 91 Income Tax Act, now Section 159 and Section 160

Readers still search the old numbers, so here are both. Under the Income-tax Act 1961, Section 90 (with Section 90A) gave relief under a treaty, which is why people look up the Section 90 Income Tax Act provisions. The Section 91 Income Tax Act provision gave relief where no treaty exists. Under the Income-tax Act 2025, which applies from 1 April 2026, treaty relief is in Section 159 and unilateral relief is in Section 160. If you are searching for the Section 159 Income Tax Act 2025 rules, they are the successor to the old Section 90.

For 2026-27 and later years, cite the new sections and forms. For earlier years, the old sections and forms still apply.

Treaty or Indian law: which applies?

You may use whichever is more beneficial: the treaty or the Act (Section 159(4), old Section 90(2)). Two conditions apply. A non-resident must hold a certificate of residence from the other country, plus the prescribed papers (Section 159(8), old Section 90(4)). And the anti-avoidance rules in the Act can still apply, because a treaty is not meant to create non-taxation.

Who can claim, and what you need

A resident of a treaty country can claim. The papers are:

  • A tax residency certificate from that country, see Tax Residency Certificate for DTAA: Who Issues It and What India Needs

  • Form 41 (old Form 10F), filed online by the non-resident, even where the certificate is held, see Form 10F (Now Form 41): When You Need It and How to File It Online

  • PAN where there is one, and proof of the income and the tax paid

  • Form 44 (old Form 67) for an Indian resident claiming foreign tax credit under Rule 76

Form 41 is a self-declaration that cannot be edited after submission. It is filed for the tax year of the claim, together with the residence certificate. Form 44 is due within 12 months from the end of the relevant tax year, and a chartered accountant verifies it for companies, or for others when the foreign tax is 1 lakh rupees or more.

Three things go wrong often:

  • The residence certificate was missing, or it was held but nothing backed it: no evidence of substance and no paper trail behind the claim.

  • The income was treated as business profits when it was really other-source income, so the wrong treaty article was relied on.

  • The Form 10F, now Form 41, was never filed.

Questions people ask about DTAA

What is the full form of DTAA?

DTAA stands for Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. It is a tax treaty between two countries. It decides which country may tax which kind of income and how the other country gives relief, so that the same income is not taxed twice. India has such treaties with many countries.

Is income exempt under a DTAA?

Usually not. A DTAA shares taxing rights between the two countries or limits the rate at which the source country can tax. Relief then comes as a credit or, in some treaties, an exemption. Some income can be taxable only in one country, but that depends on the treaty article and the income type.

What is the difference between Section 90 and Section 91, and what are they called now?

Section 90 (with 90A) gave relief under a treaty. Section 91 gave relief where no treaty exists. Under the Income-tax Act 2025, treaty relief is in Section 159 and unilateral relief is in Section 160. Use the new numbers from 2026-27, and the old numbers for earlier years.

Do I get the treaty rate automatically?

No. A non-resident must show residence in the treaty country, usually with a tax residency certificate, and give the particulars in Form 41, the new form for old Form 10F. The payer then applies the treaty rate. Without the papers, tax is usually deducted at the normal rate.

Which countries have a DTAA with India?

India has comprehensive DTAAs with more than 90 countries, and a few limited agreements covering narrower income such as shipping or air transport. The main ones for our clients are the US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Mauritius and Germany. The official list is on the Income Tax Department website.

How do I claim DTAA benefit in my ITR?

An Indian resident reports the foreign income, claims credit for foreign tax in the return, and files Form 44, the new form for old Form 67, with proof of tax paid. A non-resident claims the treaty position in the return, backed by the residency certificate and Form 41. Check the current return form.
