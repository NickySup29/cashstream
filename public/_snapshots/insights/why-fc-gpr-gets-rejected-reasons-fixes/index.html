In short

FC-GPR sent back by the AD bank? See the real reasons filings are rejected, from FIRC and KYC mismatches to valuation errors, and how to fix each.

Every FC-GPR we have filed drew at least one query from the AD bank. Most are requests for additional documents, or mismatches in the FIRC, KYC, valuation, dates or shareholding figures. A query continues the review. A rejection is final for that submission and is followed by a resubmission.

The shares are allotted, the foreign investor's money is in, and FC-GPR has been submitted. Then a message arrives from the AD bank, and the first fear is that the FC-GPR is rejected. In our files that is rarely what happened. Most of the time it is a query, and a query is a request to continue the review. Here is how to tell the two apart, what usually goes wrong, and how to fix each.

A query is not a rejection: what the AD bank can do

After you submit FC-GPR on the RBI's FIRMS portal, the authorised dealer (AD) bank reviews it. It can approve the form, raise a query, or reject it.

An FC-GPR query is a request during the review. The bank usually asks for additional documents or a clarification, and the review continues. A rejection is final for that submission and is followed by a resubmission.

In our experience the form comes back with the bank's comments, and we correct what the bank pointed out and resubmit it, without preparing the whole filing again. For the full portal steps, read How to File FC-GPR on the FIRMS Portal.

FC-GPR rejection reasons: the six we see

In our files every FC-GPR has drawn at least one query. The most common is a request from the bank for additional documents, even when the file looked complete to us. After that come mismatches between the documents and the form. The table lists the FC-GPR rejection reasons we see, with the fix for each.

No.ReasonWhy it happensThe fix
1The bank asks for additional documentsEach bank has its own checklist, and it often asks for more than the RBI's listKeep a bank-wise record of earlier queries, give the papers asked and reply promptly
2The FIRC does not match the formName, amount, date or exchange rate differEnter the amount and the rate shown on the FIRC itself
3The KYC report is incompleteThe report from the investor's bank is missing, dated or unsignedObtain a correct, dated, signed report through the Indian AD bank
4The valuation does not support the priceThe issue price is below the certified fair value, or the valuation date is far from the allotment dateCheck the issue price against the valuation certificate before filing
5The dates do not agreeAllotment, remittance and board dates differ across papersSet one date for each event and use it everywhere
6The shareholding figures do not matchShare numbers or classes differ between the resolution, the register, the MCA filing and the formReconcile the figures before submission

The first is the most common in our files. The rest are not ranked.

The FC-GPR common errors that keep coming back

The same mismatches return in different filings. Start with the FIRC: the amount, name, date and exchange rate on it must be the same as on the form. The FC-GPR KYC report from the investor's bank must be complete, dated and signed. The FC-GPR valuation certificate must support the issue price. The dates and the shareholding figures must agree across every paper.

The fix is a single reconciliation before submission. List the amount, the date, the name and the share count from every document side by side, and resolve each difference before you press submit. Most of these errors are found this way, a week earlier and at no cost.

A checklist before you submit

Our checks come from earlier submissions and a record of each bank's queries.

  1. 1

    The amount and rate on the FIRC match the form.

  2. 2

    The investor's details match across the FIRC, the KYC and the agreement.

  3. 3

    The issue price is not below the valuation certificate.

  4. 4

    Allotment, remittance and board dates agree everywhere.

  5. 5

    Share numbers and classes match the resolution, the register, the MCA filing and the form.

  6. 6

    The bank's own checklist is complete.

  7. 7

    You are filing within 30 days of allotment. If that date has passed, the late submission route applies.

FC-GPR resubmission and the deadline

If the 30 days from allotment have gone by, do not backdate or file as if on time. A late filing goes through the late submission fee route, which is available for up to three years from the due date. After that, the matter needs compounding.

An FC-GPR resubmission after a rejection matters here. If the original filing was already late, the delay keeps counting until a complete return is received, so resubmit promptly with the fix. Read Missed the RBI Deadline: Late Fee vs Compounding for the detail.
