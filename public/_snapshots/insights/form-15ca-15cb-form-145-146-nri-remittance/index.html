Form 15CA is now Form 145, the remitter's declaration filed before money goes abroad. Form 15CB is now Form 146, a chartered accountant's certificate. For an NRI property sale where the taxable remittance is above ₹5 lakh in the tax year, the sale usually falls under Part C and needs both.

The sale has closed and the buyer has paid. The money sits in the NRO account, and the bank asks for Form 15CA and 15CB before it will send a rupee abroad. Then the seller finds that neither name exists any more. This is how the new forms work, who files what, and in which order.

What changed: Form 15CA and 15CB are now Form 145 and 146

Under the Income-tax Act 2025, Form 15CA and 15CB have been renamed. Form 145 and 146 are the new forms, and the procedure sits in Rule 220 of the Income-tax Rules 2026, which corresponds to the old Rule 37BB. The idea is the same. The Department wants to confirm that tax on money going abroad has been dealt with before the bank sends it.

Old nameNew nameWho files itWhat it is
Form 15CAForm 145The person remitting, on the e-filing portalA declaration filed before the remittance goes abroad
Form 15CBForm 146A chartered accountant, after the remitter assigns the accountant on the portalA certificate on the nature of the payment and the tax position, with a UDIN

The new forms apply from 1 April 2026, when the 2025 Act took effect. In our practice, the date of the remittance decides which form you use, not the date of the sale. A remittance made before that date stays on the old forms.

Form 15CA applicability: which part of Form 145 applies?

Form 15CA applicability, now Form 145 applicability, turns on two tests. Is the remittance chargeable to tax, and is it above ₹5 lakh in the tax year? Form 145 has four parts.

PartWhen it appliesCertificateNote
ATaxable remittance, not above ₹5 lakh in the tax yearNoneRemitter's declaration only
BTaxable remittance above ₹5 lakh, with a certificate from the Assessing OfficerAssessing Officer's certificate under Section 395(1)Form 146 is not needed
CTaxable remittance above ₹5 lakh, with a certificate from a chartered accountantForm 146The usual route for a property sale
DThe remittance is not chargeable to taxNoneDeclaration only

Form 15CA Part D, now Part D of Form 145, covers a remittance that is not chargeable to tax. An NRI's property sale usually falls under Part C: the gain is taxable, the amount is above ₹5 lakh, and most sellers use a chartered accountant's certificate instead of the Assessing Officer's. If the sale gives a loss or an exempt gain, Part D may apply, but we decide that only after reviewing the computation.

Form 15CA for foreign remittance: step by step for an NRI selling property

Form 15CA for foreign remittance, now Form 145, is filed before the bank sends the money. The order is fixed: the chartered accountant first, then you, then the bank.

  1. 1

    Tax and net proceeds. The buyer deducts TDS, and the net amount lands in your NRO account. Read TDS on Sale of Property by NRI for the rates.

  2. 2

    Documents to the CA. You send the papers listed below.

  3. 3

    Assign the CA on the portal. Log in to the e-filing portal, go to My Account, choose Add CA, enter the accountant's membership number and select Form No. 146.

  4. 4

    Form 146 by the CA. The accountant prepares the certificate, signs it with a digital signature, adds the UDIN and e-verifies.

  5. 5

    Form 145 by you. You select Part C, and the acknowledgement of Form 146 prefills it. You then e-verify.

  6. 6

    The bank remits. You give the bank the acknowledgements and its own application.

Form 145 must be filed before the money goes out. It cannot be edited after it is submitted, but it can be withdrawn within 7 days of submission. One Form 146 supports only one Form 145. If your TDS is still being claimed, read How to Claim Back TDS on Property Sale.

Documents and details the CA will ask for

Keep these ready before the CA starts.

  • •

    The sale deed and any other deed copies.

  • •

    Copies of the power of attorney, if one was used.

  • •

    The will, if the property was inherited.

  • •

    Passport, PAN and Aadhaar.

  • •

    An affidavit or declaration in the format your bank asks for.

  • •

    The NRO account details and the bank's remittance application.

  • •

    The TDS certificate and proof of the tax on the gain.

  • •

    A Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41, only if a treaty benefit is claimed.

Banks differ on what else they ask for. Ask yours early.

How this fits the USD 1 million limit and the account

The forms do not set the limit. The account route does. Sale proceeds go to the NRO account, and what you can send abroad in a year depends on the route and the limit. Read NRO vs NRE for Property Sale Proceeds for that part.

Common delays and mistakes

These are the points that slow things down in our files. We do not promise any result.

  • •

    The wrong Part. Choosing Part A or D when Part C applies, or the other way round.

  • •

    Mismatched details. Names, PAN or account details that differ between the forms and the bank records.

  • •

    TDS credit not matching. The credit in your statement differs from what the forms show.

  • •

    The bank cut-off. The bank has its own deadline and queue, separate from the portal.

  • •

    CA assignment on the portal. Form 146 cannot be filed until the remitter assigns the accountant.

  • •

    Wrong information. A failure to file, or inaccurate information, can attract a penalty of up to ₹1 lakh under Section 462 of the Income-tax Act 2025.

Form 145 is the new name of Form 15CA, the remitter's declaration filed on the e-filing portal before money goes abroad. It applies to remittances made on or after 1 April 2026, under the Income-tax Act 2025 and the Income-tax Rules 2026. The core idea is unchanged, though the Parts are reorganised.

Form 145 is the declaration the remitter files on the portal before the money goes out. Form 146 is a certificate from a chartered accountant, filed first, which supports Part C of Form 145 when a taxable remittance is above ₹5 lakh in the tax year. The certificate carries a UDIN.

Form 146 is needed where the remittance is taxable, is above ₹5 lakh in the tax year, and no Assessing Officer certificate has been obtained. With such a certificate, Part B is used instead. For property sales, banks generally ask for the CA certificate, so we prepare it as a rule.

Form 146 comes first. The remitter assigns a chartered accountant on the portal, the accountant files and e-verifies Form 146, and its acknowledgement number prefills Part C of Form 145. The remitter then files Form 145, and the bank remits. One Form 146 can be used for only one Form 145.

In our files, yes, mostly. For sale proceeds, banks ask for Form 145 together with the CA certificate for FEMA and safety reasons. Requirements differ between banks, so confirm with your bank before the sale closes and keep the supporting papers ready.

Once the documents are complete, our review of the CA certificate usually takes about one week. The bank's time is separate and depends on the bank. A wrong Part, mismatched details or a TDS credit that does not match will add time. No timeline can be promised.
