India-Mauritius DTAA: What the 2024 Protocol and PPT Change

Reviewed by Cash Stream Advisors. Last reviewed: 23 November 2026.

The short answer

India and Mauritius signed their tax treaty in 1982. The 2024 Protocol, in force from 10 August 2026, adds a principal purpose test. A Mauritius tax residency certificate no longer settles the claim by itself: the Supreme Court held in January 2026 that substance and control matter. Shares bought before 1 April 2017 keep treaty protection.

For years Mauritius was the quiet route for money going into India. A tax residency certificate and a licence were usually enough. That has changed in two steps: a Supreme Court judgment in January 2026, and a new Protocol that came into force in August 2026. This guide explains what the India Mauritius DTAA says now, and what a Mauritius entity must be ready to show.

Who this affects

  • A Mauritius fund or holding company with Indian investments.

  • A foreign investor that routes India money through Mauritius.

  • An Indian company paying interest, royalties or fees to a Mauritius entity.

Grandfathering and the 2017 line

The 2016 protocol moved the taxing right on gains from Indian company shares to India for shares bought on or after 1 April 2017. Shares bought earlier kept the old Mauritius-only treatment. The CBDT said in Circular 01/2025 of 21 January 2025 that the principal purpose test does not touch that grandfathered position.

The Tiger Global judgment raised a concern that the general anti-avoidance rule could reach benefits arising after 1 April 2017 even on earlier investments. On 31 March 2026 the CBDT answered by amending the rules (Rule 10U of the 1962 Rules, and Rule 128 of the Income-tax Rules 2026): GAAR does not apply to income from the transfer of investments made before 1 April 2017. That protects the gain. It does not remove the need for substance on newer investments.

How to claim from India

  1. Mauritius tax residency certificate from the Mauritius Revenue Authority.

  2. Form 41 (old Form 10F) on the e-Filing portal. It is required even though the certificate has been issued.

  3. Beneficial ownership and substance papers, listed below.

  4. No-PE declaration if the payer asks.

  5. The payer applies the treaty rate.

The Global Business Licence

A Mauritius company that is run as a global business holds a Global Business Licence from the Financial Services Commission, alongside the tax residency certificate. Both are needed, and neither is enough alone, as Tiger Global showed.

Substance papers

Hold these before you claim:

  • Names of the office managers and the people who run the company in Mauritius.

  • Proof of expenses actually incurred there.

  • Proof that the business is operated in Mauritius, not only registered there.

  • Audited financial statements.

The principal purpose test and Tiger Global

The 2024 Protocol adds Article 27B. A benefit can be denied if it was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement, unless granting it fits the treaty's object and purpose. It is not a Multilateral Instrument covered agreement, so it applies through the Protocol itself. India notified the Protocol on 13 October 2026 (S.O. 5611(E)), and it applies from 10 August 2026 regardless of the taxable year.

In Tiger Global (Supreme Court, 15 January 2026), the Court held that a tax residency certificate is required but not conclusive. The authorities may look at where effective control and management reside, the commercial rationale and whether the entity does real economic work. The Mauritius entities held valid certificates and Category 1 Global Business Licences, yet were held not entitled to the exemption, with weight placed on the control exercised from the United States and the lack of commercial substance. The Court also held that Circular 789, which had treated the certificate as sufficient, no longer binds where abuse is alleged.

Interest, royalties and fees for Indian payers

A payer should not apply the treaty rate on a certificate alone. Check the certificate period, Form 41, the beneficial owner and the substance papers before the payment, and record the checks.

Illustration (hypothetical, not a client matter): an Indian company pays interest to a Mauritius lender that is owned by a foreign parent, with no staff or office of its own in Mauritius and decisions taken abroad. The certificate is valid. After Tiger Global, the payer and the officer would both ask who really controls the lender. A lender with real directors, an office and records in Mauritius is better placed.

Where claims go wrong

  1. Relying on the certificate alone. Fix: hold the substance papers too.

  2. A shell with no operations. Fix: show real office, people and spending in Mauritius.

  3. Control from outside Mauritius. Fix: hold board meetings and decisions there.

  4. Assuming post-2017 shares are protected like earlier ones. Fix: test substance before you claim.

  5. A Form 41 filed with errors. Fix: check it before filing, because errors can surface only at assessment.

Questions people ask about the India-Mauritius DTAA

Do India and Mauritius have a tax treaty?

Yes. The India-Mauritius Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement was signed in 1982. It was amended by a 2016 protocol that ended the capital gains exemption on shares bought from 1 April 2017, and by a Protocol signed on 7 March 2024 that adds a principal purpose test. It also caps tax on dividends, interest and royalties.

What does the 2024 Protocol change?

It inserts a principal purpose test into the treaty as Article 27B. A treaty benefit can be denied if obtaining it was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement, unless granting it would be in line with the treaty's object and purpose. It is not part of the Multilateral Instrument, so it applies through the Protocol itself.

When did the 2024 Protocol come into force?

It came into force on 10 August 2026, the later of the two countries' notifications. India notified it on 13 October 2026. It applies from that date, regardless of the taxable year. The CBDT had said earlier, in Circular 01/2025 of 21 January 2025, that the principal purpose test would apply only prospectively.

What is the principal purpose test?

It asks why an arrangement or transaction was made. If obtaining a treaty benefit was one of its principal purposes, the benefit can be denied, unless granting it fits the treaty's object and purpose. Commercial reasons, real operations and decisions taken in Mauritius help show that the benefit was not the main aim.

Are shares bought before 1 April 2017 still protected?

Yes. The 2016 protocol grandfathered them, and the CBDT said in Circular 01/2025 that the principal purpose test does not touch that position. After the Tiger Global ruling, the CBDT also amended the rules in March 2026 so that GAAR does not apply to income from transferring such investments.

Is a Mauritius tax residency certificate enough?

No. The Supreme Court held in Tiger Global on 15 January 2026 that the certificate is required but not conclusive. The authorities can examine where control and management sit, the commercial rationale and whether the entity performs real economic functions. A valid certificate and licence did not save the Mauritius entities in that case.

Which form do I file in India to claim the Mauritius treaty?

Form 41 (old Form 10F) on the Income Tax e-Filing portal, together with the Mauritius tax residency certificate. The Indian payer applies the treaty rate on the strength of both and a no-PE declaration. Keep the substance papers separately, because an officer can ask for them later, even years after the filing.
