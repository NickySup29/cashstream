PAN, passport or residency proof, contracts for cross-border transactions, Tax Residency Certificate, Form 41, financial statements, TDS certificates or Form 26AS, and prior year filings. The exact list varies by service, each individual service page has the specifics for your situation.

Common pitfalls

Mistakes we see most often

Treating It As an Afterthought

Cross-border tax planned after the transaction, instead of before it, when far fewer options remain.

Missing Procedural Requirements

Being technically eligible for a benefit but missing the TRC, Form 41, or Form 145/146 that's needed to actually claim it.

DIY Treaty Interpretation

Small differences in treaty article or fact pattern change the outcome entirely, this is rarely a safe area to self-serve.

No Documentation Ready

Cross-border filings get more scrutiny, not less. Documentation gathered after a notice arrives is a much harder position.

What's at stake

What happens if this isn't handled correctly

  • →Double taxation, blocked refunds, penalty and interest
  • →Compliance notices and prolonged litigation
  • →Cash flow issues from excess TDS sitting unclaimed
  • →Reputational and legal risk on future cross-border dealings
