FC-GPR vs FC-TRS vs FLA Return: Which RBI Form Applies to You?

Reflects Form 128 under the Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026.

FC-GPR reports a fresh issue of shares to a foreign investor. FC-TRS reports a transfer of shares between a resident and a non-resident. The FLA return is the yearly return of foreign assets and liabilities. They are different forms, and a company can owe more than one in a year; in our files, at most two.

FC-GPR vs FC-TRS vs FLA return is a question we hear often, because all three go to the RBI and all three sound alike. They are not the same, and a company can owe more than one in a year.

The three forms at a glance

FC-GPR is filed by the company, FC-TRS by the resident party to a transfer, and the FLA return by the company every year. The table shows the trigger, the filer, the deadline and the portal.

What the names stand for

FC-GPR full form: Foreign Currency, Gross Provisional Return. FC-TRS full form: Foreign Currency, Transfer of Shares. FLA return stands for Foreign Liabilities and Assets. In practice the FC-TRS is the report of a share transfer, and the FC-GPR is the report of a fresh issue.

The difference between FC-GPR and FC-TRS

The difference between FC-GPR and FC-TRS is who is moving the shares. In FC-GPR the company issues new shares to a foreign investor. In FC-TRS an existing holder sells to someone on the other side of the resident and non-resident line. Same investor, same company, but two different forms.

Which one are you?

Match your situation below. These are examples, not cases from our files.

  1. 1

    Your company issues shares to a foreign investor. File FC-GPR, within 30 days of allotment.

  2. 2

    A resident shareholder sells shares to a non-resident. File FC-TRS, as the resident transferor.

  3. 3

    A non-resident shareholder sells shares to a resident. File FC-TRS, as the resident transferee.

  4. 4

    Your company has foreign investment on its books at 31 March. File the FLA return by 15 July.

  5. 5

    Two things happen in a year, for example an issue to a foreign investor and the year-end return. File FC-GPR and the FLA return. They do not cancel each other.

Sector conditions can change what is allowed for a given industry, so check the rules for your sector before you file.

Three mix-ups to avoid

  1. 1

    Thinking the FLA return replaces the others. It does not. The FLA return is annual compliance. FC-GPR and FC-TRS report each issue or transfer when it happens.

  2. 2

    Thinking company law filings cover RBI reporting. Filings with the registrar are separate from the reports to the RBI.

  3. 3

    Filing the wrong form for a transfer. An issue by the company is FC-GPR, and a transfer between holders is FC-TRS.

If you filed the wrong form, file the correct one afresh. The late submission fee route is available for up to three years from the due date, after which compounding applies.

FC-GPR reports a fresh issue of shares by the company to a foreign investor, and the company files it. FC-TRS reports a transfer of shares between a resident and a non-resident, and the resident transferor or transferee files it. FC-GPR is due within 30 days of allotment, FC-TRS within 60 days.

No. The FLA return is the yearly return of foreign assets and liabilities, filed by the company by 15 July. It does not replace FC-GPR or FC-TRS, which report each issue or transfer as it happens. A change in shareholders through a fresh issue still needs FC-GPR in that year.

In our files a company files at most two in a year: the FLA return, which is annual, plus FC-GPR when it issues shares to a foreign investor. FC-TRS is filed by the resident party to a share transfer, not necessarily the company, so all three in one year would be unusual.

FC-GPR and FC-TRS are reported on the RBI's FIRMS portal, through the AD bank. The FLA return is filed on the RBI's FLAIR portal. The deadlines differ: 30 days from allotment for FC-GPR, 60 days for FC-TRS, and 15 July each year for the FLA return.

File the correct form afresh. If the due date has passed, the late submission fee applies, and the late submission route is available for up to three years from the due date, after which compounding is needed. Check which form your transaction needs, and ask your AD bank to confirm.
