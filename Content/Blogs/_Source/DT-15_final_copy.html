Permanent Establishment Under a Tax Treaty: Test, Risk and Profit Attribution

Reviewed by Cash Stream Advisors. Last reviewed: 23 November 2026.

The short answer

A permanent establishment is a fixed place, a dependent agent, a service team or a long project through which a foreign company does business in India. Under a treaty, India can tax the company's business profits only to the extent they are attributable to it. Without one, India generally cannot tax those profits.

A German engineering firm sends two people to Pune for a commissioning job that was meant to take six weeks. Months later, the Indian customer asks for a no-PE certificate before paying the final invoice. The question behind it is simple: has the firm, without meaning to, set up a permanent establishment in India? This guide explains the permanent establishment test, the risk, and how profit is attributed.

Who this affects

  • A foreign group with staff, an agent or a project in India.

  • An Indian company hosting a foreign team or contracting with a foreign vendor.

  • A CA firm reviewing an exposure.

The PE tests and the usual thresholds

There are five ways a PE arises. The permanent establishment test and its threshold depend on the treaty.

TypeTestUsual threshold
Fixed placeA place at the enterprise's disposal, through which business is carried onNo fixed time limit in most treaties; the facts decide
Dependent agentA person who habitually concludes contracts, or secures ordersFacts
Service PEServices through employees or other personnelDays in a period, by treaty
Construction or installationA site or project beyond the treaty periodBy treaty, for example six months in Germany and the UK
Preparatory or auxiliaryExcluded if genuinely only preparatoryWatch for fragmenting one business into small parts

The permanent establishment meaning in practice: a fixed place can be an office, a desk at a customer's plant, or a hotel run by a foreign company. A dependent agent is not independent: it works mainly for the foreign company. The exclusion for preparatory work is lost where the activity is the core of the business.

What the courts have said recently

  • Hyatt (Supreme Court, 24 July 2025). A Dubai company with strategic oversight agreements with hotels in India was held to have a fixed place PE under Article 5(1) of the India-UAE treaty. The Court said there is no standard formula, and looked at the company's control over day-to-day operations and its disposal of the premises through its employees.

  • Hyatt (Delhi High Court Full Bench, 2024). A PE in India is taxable on income attributable to it even where the enterprise as a whole made a loss, because Article 7 treats the PE as a distinct enterprise. Reported at (2024) 340 CTR 633.

  • Clifford Chance (Delhi High Court, 4 December 2025). Under Article 5(6)(a) of the India-Singapore treaty, a service PE needs physical presence in India. The Court rejected a "virtual service PE", and found that after excluding vacation and business development days, the firm's staff were in India for about 44 days, below the 90 days. ITA 353 and 354 of 2025.

How profit is attributed to a PE

India taxes only the profit attributable to the PE, as if the PE were a separate and independent enterprise. That is the rule in Article 7(2) of most treaties.

Attribution has three steps. First, a functions, assets and risks analysis: what the PE does in India, what assets it uses, what risks it carries. Second, a method: where income cannot be worked out accurately, the officer can estimate it on a reasonable basis, for example by a percentage of turnover, or by apportioning global profit using India's share of receipts, under the methods in the Income-tax Rules (old Rule 10). Third, a computation and filing.

This is not transfer pricing. Transfer pricing prices transactions between related parties. Attribution decides how much profit belongs to the PE. The two can overlap but are separate exercises.

After the Full Bench in Hyatt, a loss-making foreign group cannot assume there is no Indian profit.

Illustration (hypothetical, not a client matter): a European contractor completes a project in India through a project office. Without an attribution study, the department could attribute a large share of the whole contract value as Indian profit. A study of what the project office did, which assets it used and what risks it bore supports a smaller share.

What having a PE costs and what to file

A PE is taxed as a foreign company. The tax rate for permanent establishment in India on business profits was a base rate of 35% for 2025-26, plus surcharge of up to 5% and 4% cess, which gives an effective rate of about 36.4% to 38.2%, as summarised by PwC. Confirm the rate for the year in question.

You will usually need:

  • a PAN and a TAN;

  • an income tax return in Form ITR-6;

  • books of account and, where required, a tax audit;

  • transfer pricing documentation where there are related-party dealings;

  • TDS compliance where the PE pays others.

No PE certificate: what it is and when a payer asks for it

A no permanent establishment certificate is a declaration by the foreign company that it has no permanent establishment in India. Indian payers ask for it with the tax residency certificate and Form 41 before they pay at a treaty rate. It should say who the company is, which treaty applies, the period, and that it has no PE, and the facts should support it. A declaration that does not match the facts can be used against the company later. See Form 10F (Now Form 41): When You Need It and How to File It Online.

How to manage the risk

Our first questions to a client are:

  1. Do you have an office, or a desk, anywhere in India?

  2. Who is in India, for how long, and what do they do?

  3. Does anyone here negotiate or conclude contracts for you?

  4. Is there a project, and when does it start and end?

  5. Does the Indian company or a subsidiary act for you?

Then route contracts so they are signed outside India, keep a day log of visits, and choose between a subsidiary and a branch with the tax result in mind. Keep the contracts, the day log and the project records. On permanent establishment risk, the day log is often the first thing an officer asks for.

On digital taxation, the significant economic presence rule in Indian law can tax a non-resident without a PE where thresholds of revenue or users are crossed, and a treaty may still protect it.

Questions people ask about permanent establishment

What is a permanent establishment in India?

It is a presence through which a foreign company carries on business in India, such as a fixed place of business, a long construction project, a service team staying beyond a set number of days, or a dependent agent who concludes contracts. If one exists, India can tax the profits attributable to it. The exact test depends on the treaty.

How many days create a service PE?

It depends on the treaty. The US and UK treaties use more than 90 days in a 12-month period, and Singapore uses more than 90 days in a fiscal year, with 30 days for services to related enterprises in the UK and Singapore. Germany has no general service PE clause. Check the treaty with the home country of the company.

Does one employee in India create a PE?

Not automatically. One employee can create a fixed place PE if the office is at the company's disposal and the business is carried on through it, or can create a dependent agent PE if the employee habitually concludes contracts. A short stay under a service PE threshold may not. The facts and the treaty decide.

How is profit attributed to a PE?

The PE is treated as a separate and independent enterprise, and only the profit attributable to it is taxed in India, under Article 7. A functions, assets and risks analysis is used, and where income cannot be worked out accurately, the officer may use a method under the rules, such as a percentage of turnover.

Is a PE taxed if the foreign company makes a global loss?

Yes, it can be. The Delhi High Court Full Bench in Hyatt (2024) held that profits attributable to the Indian PE are taxed as if the PE were an independent entity, so global losses do not remove the tax. The Supreme Court affirmed the PE finding in 2025. Attribution looks at what the PE earns in India.

Is a subsidiary a permanent establishment?

Not by itself. A subsidiary is a separate company, and its presence does not automatically create a PE for the foreign parent. A PE can arise if the parent controls the subsidiary's premises, or the subsidiary acts as a dependent agent concluding contracts for the parent. Court rulings turn on the facts.

What is a no permanent establishment certificate?

It is a declaration from the foreign company that it has no permanent establishment in India, which an Indian payer often asks for with the tax residency certificate and Form 41 before it pays at the treaty rate or nil. It should match the facts, because a wrong declaration can be challenged later.
