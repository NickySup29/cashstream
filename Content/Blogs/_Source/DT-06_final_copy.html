India-UK DTAA: Treaty Rates, Pensions and How to Claim Them

Reviewed by Cash Stream Advisors. Last reviewed: 23 November 2026.

A UK pension that a returning Indian is unsure how to report. A flat in Chennai whose rent the owner in London is taxed on twice. A UK vendor whose invoice an Indian payer will not release without papers. All three come back to one document. So what does the India UK DTAA actually say, and what do you have to show before it helps you?

You claim it with a UK certificate of residence and, in India, Form 41 (old Form 10F). For the papers, read Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) for DTAA: Who Issues It and What India Needs and Form 10F (Now Form 41): When You Need It and How to File It Online.

People search for it under several names: the India UK tax treaty, the UK India double taxation treaty, or the DTAA between India and UK. They all mean the same convention.

Who this affects

  • A UK-based NRI with India income such as rent, interest or dividends.

  • A returning Indian with a UK pension or UK property, moving back to India.

  • An Indian company paying a UK vendor for royalties, services or software.

Rates at a glance

A treaty rate is a cap on India's tax, not the tax you pay. The payer deducts at the lower of the treaty and domestic rate, and surcharge and cess are not added to a treaty rate. For all countries side by side, read India DTAA Rates and Countries: Dividend, Interest, Royalty and Fees.

Interest paid to the UK government, a political subdivision or a UK local authority is exempt in India, as is certain interest on loans guaranteed or insured by UK Export Finance.

Pensions and rental income

The pension articles work in both directions.

  • UK pension, person resident in India. A private pension or annuity is taxable only in India (Article 20(1)). A UK government service pension is taxable only in the UK (Article 19(2)). Example: a returning Indian who worked in the private sector draws a UK pension. India taxes it as a resident, and the UK should not tax it once treaty relief is claimed.

  • Indian pension, person resident in the UK. A private pension or annuity is taxable only in the UK (Article 20(1)). An Indian government service pension is taxable only in India (Article 19(2)). Example: a UK resident draws a pension from a private Indian employer. It is taxed in the UK, not in India.

  • Rental income from Indian property. India may tax it, because the property is there (Article 6(1)). A UK-resident owner is also taxed in the UK on worldwide income, and the UK gives credit for the Indian tax (Article 24). Example: a UK resident owns a flat in Chennai. India taxes the rent, and the UK credits that tax.

Article 20(2) treats payments under social security legislation as pensions, so a UK State Pension paid to a resident of India should fall under the same rule. The way a UK pension is reported in the UK, and any relief claim there, needs a UK adviser. For NRI rental income, read the NRI pages on this site.

How to claim from India

  1. Get the HMRC certificate of residence for the period of the claim.

  2. File Form 41 (old Form 10F) on the Income Tax e-Filing portal. It is required even though the certificate has been issued.

  3. Give the payer the papers before payment: the certificate, Form 41, your PAN or tax identification number, and a no-PE declaration if asked.

  4. The payer applies the treaty rate before it deducts tax.

  5. For an NRI with an Indian bank, the bank may ask for its own declaration alongside these papers.

How to claim from the UK

We explain two things on the UK side: how the credit works and what the certificate of residence is. Under Article 24 of the treaty, Indian tax on income from Indian sources is allowed as a credit against UK tax on the same profits. The credit is limited to the UK tax on that income, so Indian tax does not erase UK tax. An Indian resident who needs relief in the UK first obtains a certificate of residence in India (Form 42, with the certificate issued in Form 43).

The UK self assessment return, including the foreign pages and the credit entries, should be prepared by a UK adviser. We do not prepare UK returns.

The MLI and the principal purpose test

The Multilateral Instrument modifies this treaty. A principal purpose test replaced the treaty's Article 28C. It applies to UK withholding taxes from 1 January 2020, and to Indian withholding taxes from 1 April 2020. A benefit can be denied where obtaining it was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement, unless granting it fits the object of the treaty. The certificate alone does not guarantee the rate.

Capital gains between India and the UK

Article 14 lets each country tax capital gains under its own law. So India may tax a gain on Indian property or shares, and the UK may tax the same gain on a UK resident, with credit given. The tax depends on the asset, the holding period and your residential status in India. Speak to us before you sell if the amount is large.

Where claims go wrong

Five common mistakes, with fixes:

  1. Certificate does not cover the income year. Fix: match the period before the first payment.

  2. No Form 41, or filed late. Fix: file it before the payment.

  3. Using the 10% rate on the wrong payment. The lower rate is for equipment and ancillary services. Fix: check what the payment is for.

  4. Treating a pension as taxable in both countries. Fix: read Articles 19 and 20 before the return is filed.

  5. No UK credit claimed. Fix: keep the Indian tax certificate and give it to your UK adviser.

Questions people ask about the India-UK DTAA

Do India and the UK have a tax treaty?

Yes. The India-UK Double Taxation Convention was signed on 25 January 1993 and came into force on 25 October 1993. A protocol in force from 27 December 2013 amended it, and the Multilateral Instrument modifies it from 2020. It caps source-country tax on dividends, interest, royalties and fees, and both countries give credit for tax paid.

What are the India-UK DTAA rates on dividends, interest and royalties?

Dividends are capped at 10%, or 15% for property-income dividends. Interest is 15%, or 10% for a bank. Royalties and fees for technical services are 15%, or 10% for equipment and ancillary services. These are caps on India's tax, not the tax you pay, and were checked on 23 November 2026.

Is a UK pension taxable in India?

For a person resident in India, a private UK pension is taxable only in India under Article 20 of the treaty, so you should claim relief from UK tax. A UK government service pension is taxable only in the UK under Article 19. India taxes a resident on worldwide income, subject to residential status.

How does a UK-based NRI claim the India-UK DTAA?

Give the Indian payer or bank your HMRC certificate of residence and Form 41 before tax is deducted, with your PAN and a no-PE declaration where asked. India then applies the treaty rate. In the UK, you report the Indian income and claim credit for Indian tax. Keep the acknowledgement and certificate on file.

What documents are needed to claim treaty benefits?

Usually the HMRC certificate of residence for the right period, Form 41 filed on the e-Filing portal, your PAN or tax identification number, and a no-PE declaration where the payer asks. A UK company should also be ready to show substance in the UK and, if the payer asks, an auditor's certified copy confirming residency.

Can I get credit in the UK for tax paid in India?

Yes. Under Article 24 of the treaty, Indian tax on income from Indian sources is allowed as a credit against UK tax on the same profits. The credit cannot exceed the UK tax on that income. It is claimed through the UK return, so a UK adviser should prepare it.

Does the MLI change the India-UK treaty?

Yes, in one respect. The MLI modifies the treaty and adds a principal purpose test, effective from 1 January 2020 for UK withholding taxes and 1 April 2020 for Indian ones. The rates did not change. But a benefit can be denied where obtaining it was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement.
