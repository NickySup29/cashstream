The problem

Not sure whether you're NRI, RNOR, or Resident this year?

Residency status isn't decided once, it's reassessed every single financial year, based on where you actually spent your time. Getting it wrong in either direction has real consequences.

Claiming NRI status incorrectly

Miscounting days in India, or missing a special test that applies to higher-income Indian citizens, can mean a status you assumed was safe gets reassessed later, with interest and penalty attached.

Missing the RNOR planning window

Returning NRIs often qualify for a limited, valuable transitional period where foreign income stays largely outside Indian tax. Poor timing of the move can shrink or waste that window entirely.

What is NRI & relocation advisory

Guidance for anyone moving into, or out of, India

Your Indian tax residency status, Non-Resident, Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), or Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR), determines whether India taxes only your Indian income, or your income worldwide. It's assessed fresh for every tax year, based on physical presence and a small number of special rules.

This service covers both directions: NRIs returning to India permanently and planning their RNOR window, and Indian residents relocating abroad who need to understand what happens to their India income, accounts, and disclosure obligations along the way.

At Cash Stream Advisors, we assess your residency position, plan the timing of your move where that's still possible, and handle the ongoing filing, whichever direction you're headed.

The RNOR window is usually available for about two years, and it's the single biggest planning opportunity most returning NRIs miss.

Income Tax Act 2025 Update

The residency and RNOR rules that decide your status have not changed under the Income Tax Act, 2025, they remain at Section 6, unchanged in substance and in number. A few related provisions have been renumbered, effective 1 April 2026:

  • →Sections 90 and 90A (DTAA agreements and adoption) are now Section 159
  • →Section 91 (relief where no DTAA exists) is now Section 160, a separate section from 159
  • →Form 15CA and Form 15CB (used for repatriating funds abroad) are now Form 145 and Form 146

References to the old numbers in earlier correspondence should be read accordingly.

The question that decides everything

Which Category Are You?

Your status for the year decides what India can tax, it isn't a single test, but most situations fall into one of three categories.

Non-Resident (NRI)

Under 182 Days in India

Generally applies if you’re in India for fewer than 182 days in the tax year, and fewer than 60 days if you spent 365 days or more here across the prior four years. Only your Indian-source income is taxed.

RNOR

A Transitional Status

Often applies to returning NRIs, if you were a non-resident in 9 of the last 10 years, or in India for 729 days or fewer over the past 7 years. Foreign income generally stays outside Indian tax during this window.

Resident & Ordinarily Resident

Worldwide Income Taxed

Applies once you’re a resident and don’t qualify for RNOR, typically after settling back in India for a few years. Your global income becomes taxable in India from this point.

What's at stake

What happens if this isn't handled correctly

  • →A wrong residency claim leads to reassessment and penalty
  • →Non-disclosure of foreign assets can trigger exposure under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015, a separate law with its own penalties
  • →Poor timing means losing RNOR tax benefits that don’t come back
  • →Double taxation, from poor planning between two countries
  • →Funds blocked from repatriation due to account or documentation issues
Common pitfalls

Mistakes we see most often

Miscounting Days in India

Wrongly claiming NRI status based on an inaccurate day count, one of the most common and most costly errors.

Poor Move Timing

Not planning the exact date of relocation, and losing part or all of the RNOR benefit window as a result.

Mixing NRE and NRO Funds

Combining the two account types causes real repatriation problems later, they’re not interchangeable.

Skipping Foreign Asset Disclosure

Residents who don’t disclose foreign assets and income in the FA schedule of their ITR take on real, avoidable risk.

Ignoring Exit Compliance

Leaving India permanently without addressing exit tax and compliance obligations before departure.
