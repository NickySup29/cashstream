How Long Does a Lower Deduction Certificate Take?

A lower deduction certificate usually takes about 40 to 45 days from filing Form 128 on TRACES. In our cases the fastest took 15 to 20 days and the slowest took 75 days. Older CBDT guidance set a one month target, but our typical case runs longer, so apply two to three months before the first payment.

Why does the timing matter so much?

The payment date is fixed, the buyer or payer is ready, and the certificate has not arrived. Without it, the payer deducts tax at the normal rate, and the extra amount is stuck until a refund. That is why people ask how long a lower deduction certificate takes before they file.

This guide shows the time at each stage, what causes delay, what helps, and what to do if the payment date comes first. For the form change itself, read Form 128 vs Form 13.

How long does each stage take?

StageWhat happensTypical time in our cases
FilingApplication filed on TRACES and e-verifiedDay 0
First queryThe Assessing Officer asks for the computation, the nature of the transactions and supporting documentsAbout 5 days after filing
Your replyYou upload the reply and papers on the portalAbout 3 days, or the same day if you are ready
More queriesTwo rounds of queries are typicalEach round adds time
CertificateApproved and available to download on TRACESAbout 40 to 45 days in total

Our fastest cases came through in 15 to 20 days and the slowest took 75 days. Extra rounds of queries and the officer's workload are what move an application along that range.

Is there a time limit for the officer to decide?

CBDT Instruction No. 1/2014 of 15 January 2014 told Assessing Officers to follow the Citizens Charter and decide on Section 197 (now Section 395(1)) applications for nil or lower deduction within one month. Source: CBDT Instruction No. 1/2014, reproduced by BCAJ.

In practice, treat one month as a target. Our typical case runs longer, so plan for 40 to 45 days at least.

What causes a lower deduction certificate to be delayed?

  • •Incomplete computation. An incorrect or incomplete computation is the most common cause of repeat queries.
  • •Vague answers. A short reply that does not answer the officer's question brings another query.
  • •Reply by email only. The reply must be uploaded on the portal, not only sent by email.
  • •Missing papers. Proof of cost or an exemption claim that is not attached leads to repeat queries.
  • •Officer workload. An application can move to another officer when a charge is reassigned or an officer takes an additional charge, which resets the pace.

What do officers usually ask?

Most queries fall into three groups: the computation of income and tax, the nature of the transactions, and supporting documents for the transaction. Keep all three ready before you file. Applications are rarely rejected, and a rejection usually follows when the information asked for is not given.

How can I speed it up?

  • •Reply to every query the same day, and upload the reply on the portal.
  • •Follow up consistently with the TDS Assessing Officer.
  • •Ask for expedited disposal when the payment date is close.
  • •Keep checking the status on the portal.

These help, but no one can promise a date. The safest step is to apply early.

What statuses will I see on TRACES?

StatusWhat it means
SubmittedYour application is filed
Query raisedThe officer has asked for information. Reply on the portal
Under processingThe officer is reviewing the application or your reply
Approved or rejectedThe certificate is issued, or the application is closed

What if the certificate is late and the payment date is close?

The payer deducts tax at the normal applicable rates, and the seller claims the excess back through the income tax return. A certificate cannot reduce tax that has already been deducted, because it must reach the payer before deduction. Where the contract allows, the payer can hold the next payment until the certificate arrives.

How early should I apply?

Apply two to three months before the first payment, whether it is a property sale or a payment to a foreign company. The typical time is 40 to 45 days, and officer queries need room.
