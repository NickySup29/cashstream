What this service covers

The last stage where facts still get argued

The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal is the second appellate authority, and the last one where facts are still actively argued. After ITAT, appeals to the High Court and Supreme Court are generally restricted to substantial questions of law, not fresh factual argument. That makes this stage disproportionately important: whatever record exists when ITAT rules is largely the record you're stuck with going forward.

We prepare the paper book, draft the legal submissions, and represent you at the Tribunal, coordinating with tax counsel where the matter calls for it, or representing directly where the matter is Chartered Accountant-eligible.

ITAT is your last real chance to argue facts. A strong paper book, prepared early and organized properly, matters more than a longer hearing.
Income Tax Act, 2025 update

Which Act actually applies to your appeal

The same transition logic that applies to notices and CIT(A) appeals applies here. Section 536 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 keeps the old Income-tax Act, 1961 governing proceedings relating to a tax year that began before 1 April 2026, and since an ITAT appeal only exists because a CIT(A) order for a past year already exists, your appeal is filed under Section 253, using Form 36, under the old Act, for the foreseeable future.

The most-asked question

What's a paper book, and why it decides cases

This is one of the most-asked questions from clients heading into ITAT, and it deserves a real answer, not a one-line definition.

01

What it actually is

An indexed, complete compilation of everything relevant to your case, the return, submissions made at every earlier stage, evidence, correspondence, and the orders themselves. Not a folder of documents, a structured record the Tribunal can actually navigate.

02

Why it matters more than the hearing

ITAT is a fact-finding forum, and facts are established through the record, not through how persuasively someone speaks on the day. A well-organized paper book makes your strongest points easy to find.

03

Why it has to happen early

Gaps in the paper book surface as gaps in the argument. Finding them the week of the hearing leaves no time to fix them; finding them months earlier means they simply don't become a problem.

04

What we do differently

We treat paper book preparation as its own discipline, not a clerical step before the "real" legal work. Tribunal outcomes often turn on how well the record is organized.

Missing the Deadline or Filing an Unindexed Paper Book

Engaging Counsel Too Late

Not planning cash flow or business decisions around a multi-year timeline.

Poor Coordination Between CA and Counsel

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an ITAT appeal typically take?+
It varies significantly with case complexity, the Tribunal's current pendency, and how contested the issues are. Cases involving transfer pricing or complex legal questions generally take longer than straightforward factual disputes.
What is the time limit for filing an ITAT appeal?+
Under the currently applicable old Act, generally 60 days from the date the CIT(A) or JCIT(A) order is communicated to you. This is a hard deadline, and appeals filed physically rather than electronically are no longer accepted.
Do I need a separate advocate, or can my CA represent me?+
For many matters, a Chartered Accountant can represent you directly at ITAT. For genuinely complex legal questions, we brief and coordinate with tax counsel rather than leaving that coordination to happen informally or too late.
Can the Department appeal against a favorable CIT(A) order?+
Yes. If the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner considers the relief granted erroneous, the Department can file its own appeal, and that order then needs active defense.
What are my options if I lose at ITAT?+
Since ITAT is the last fact-finding forum, further appeal to the High Court is generally restricted to substantial questions of law, not fresh factual argument. This is exactly why the paper book and factual record built at this stage matter as much as they do.
What is a cross objection, and do I need to file one?+
If the other side, often the Department, has appealed an order, you can file a cross objection to raise your own grievances against the same order without needing a separate, independent appeal. It's typically due within a short window of receiving notice of the other party's appeal.
Can I still file an ITAT appeal if I've missed the deadline?+
Sometimes, through a delay condonation application, if you can show sufficient cause for the delay. This isn't guaranteed, and the strength of your explanation matters, but a missed deadline isn't automatically the end of the matter.
Do I need to pay the disputed demand while my ITAT appeal is pending?+
There's generally no mandatory pre-deposit requirement at the ITAT stage itself, but a stay of demand isn't automatic either, it typically needs a separate application if recovery proceedings are a concern.
How much does it cost to file an ITAT appeal?+
The filing fee itself is prescribed based on the disputed amount involved, separate from our professional fees for handling the matter. We confirm both once we've reviewed your case.
