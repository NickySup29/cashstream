Sold Your Property at a Loss? How a Lower Deduction Certificate Can Cut the TDS

Yes, the buyer still deducts TDS on the full sale price even when you sell at a loss, because the buyer does not know your cost. A lower or nil deduction certificate from the Income Tax Department can reduce that deduction, but only if the officer accepts your computation of the loss or gain and the proof behind it.

Why TDS hits the full price even when you made a loss

The buyer deducts TDS on the whole sale value, not on your profit. The law asks the buyer to withhold tax at the time of payment, and the buyer cannot check what you paid for the property, what you spent on it, or whether you made a gain. That is why TDS on property sale no capital gain cases still bite, and why TDS on sale of property at loss is a real cash problem.

For a resident seller, the deduction is 1% of the full price where the price is ₹50 lakh or more. That is small and usually settles itself when you file your return.

For an NRI seller, the picture is very different. The buyer deducts at the full capital gains rate, plus surcharge and cess, on the entire sale value. On a long term sale that is 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, and more on a short term sale. On a ₹1 crore flat, about ₹14 lakh can be held back even if you made no gain.

You can claim the excess back through your return, but the refund takes months. A certificate deals with the problem before the money is deducted.

Can you still get a certificate if you sold at a loss?

Searching for TDS on sale of property at loss lower deduction certificate? The short answer is yes. You can apply for a lower deduction certificate for sale of property at a loss, and a loss is one of the clearer cases for it. What decides the result is not the loss alone.

The application is made on Form 128 under Section 395 of the Income-tax Act 2025, which replaced Form 13 and Section 197 from 1 April 2026. You file it on TRACES, the department's TDS portal, before the buyer makes the payment.

The officer looks at three things: whether your cost and improvement figures are backed by documents, whether the working of the loss or gain is correct, and your overall tax position for the year, including other income and pending demands.

A nil TDS certificate property sale outcome is the exception: about 2 in 10 of our loss cases. The rest carried a lower rate. In one sale at a loss of about ₹12 lakh, the officer granted 2.5%, not nil. No one can promise the outcome, so apply early, ideally as soon as the sale terms are agreed. For the nil and lower distinction, read Nil vs Lower TDS Certificate.

Loss, low gain and normal gain: how each is treated

How much a certificate helps depends on what your computation shows. In each case the buyer starts with TDS on the full sale price. What changes is how far a certificate can bring that down.

CaseWhat the computation showsWhat the certificate can change
LossSale value is below your cost of acquisition, improvement and transfer costs. No tax is payable on this sale.You can ask for nil deduction, or a low rate if you have other taxable income. The officer looks hardest at the cost proof.
Low gainA small gain against a large sale value, so the real tax is a small fraction of the price.The rate can be set on the actual tax due. In our ₹7 crore case it was 5.5%, against 14.95% as standard.
Normal gainA meaningful gain, taxed at the normal capital gains rate.The saving is smaller, but a certificate still helps where an exemption such as reinvestment in a new house applies.

Do the working properly before you apply. If the sale price is below the stamp duty value, the gain is computed on the stamp duty value beyond a small tolerance, so a loss on paper may not survive. For residents who bought before 23 July 2024, the choice between 20% with indexation and 12.5% without it can turn a small gain into a loss. That choice is open only to resident individuals and HUFs, not NRIs.

A capital loss on sale of property is not wasted either. A long term capital loss can be carried forward for eight years and set off against future long term gains, provided you file your return on time.

What the officer needs to see

The officer needs enough to check your working without coming back to you. For a Section 197 certificate capital loss case (now Section 395), our complete file covers the following.

  • •

    Sale value: the agreement or draft deed showing price, payment schedule and buyer details, with the buyer's PAN and TAN where available.

  • •

    Cost and proof: the purchase deed, builder agreement or allotment letter, with payment receipts and bank statements. Stamp duty and registration charges form part of the cost.

  • •

    Improvement cost: invoices and payment proof for structural work, extensions or major renovation. Routine repairs and furnishing usually do not count.

  • •

    Transfer costs: brokerage, legal fees and other sale costs, with bills.

  • •

    The working: a clear computation of the loss or gain, showing each figure and its source.

  • •

    Your tax position: PAN, returns and computations for the preceding four years, an estimate of income for the current year, and details of any pending demands.

  • •

    For NRIs: passport and visa pages or other proof of residential status, and your overseas address.

Where the deed is old or some payments are undocumented, expect closer questions. For the full list, read Documents Needed for a Lower Deduction Certificate.

If the certificate does not come in time

If the deed is signed and paid for before the certificate arrives, the buyer has to deduct TDS at the full rate. A certificate cannot be applied to a payment already made.

The excess is not lost. You claim it back as a refund in your return for the year, showing the loss or low gain. The refund follows processing, which can take several months. If timing is tight, ask the buyer whether the final payment can wait a few weeks. For the claim, read How to Claim Back TDS on Property Sale.
