In short

Who must file the RBI FLA return, the 15 July due date, what to do if audited accounts are not ready, and what happens if you miss it.

The FLA return is an annual return to the RBI for Indian entities with outstanding foreign direct investment or overseas direct investment at 31 March. It is due by 15 July each year and is filed on the RBI's FLAIR portal. Companies, LLPs and others, such as AIFs and partnership firms, must file.

Who must file the FLA return?

An entity must file if it has outstanding foreign direct investment (FDI) or overseas direct investment (ODI) at the end of March, in the reporting year or the year before. Use this as a checklist:

  • •

    Companies with foreign investors on the register, or with investments abroad.

  • •

    LLPs with foreign capital or overseas investments.

  • •

    Others, such as SEBI-registered AIFs, partnership firms, proprietorships and public-private partnerships, with outstanding FDI or ODI.

  • •

    The previous year counts. An entity with outstanding investment in either year files, even if the investment was exited during the year.

If you hold a foreign investment but are unsure whether it counts, ask before 15 July, not after.

Who does not need to file

The RBI's FAQ lists these cases:

  • •

    no outstanding FDI or ODI at end-March of either year

  • •

    share application money only, with no allotment

  • •

    shares issued to non-residents on a non-repatriable basis, which are not counted as foreign investment

  • •

    entities regulated in an IFSC, which do not file the FLA return with the RBI

A company that thinks it is exempt should still check.

By when, and what if the accounts are not ready

The FLA return due date is 15 July each year, for the position at 31 March. For FY 2025-26 the RBI extended the date to 31 July 2026. Check for an extension in the year you file.

If the accounts are not audited in time, file on provisional or unaudited figures by the due date, and revise once the audit is done.

A revision needs approval. The RBI's FAQ says it is sought through the FLAIR portal, and without approval a revised return cannot be filed. In our files the RBI generally allows a revision within a year of the original filing if you write to its support contact, and beyond that it depends on the facts. Check the FLAIR portal for the current support email before you write.

How it is filed

On the RBI's FLAIR portal (flair.rbi.org.in), which is different from the FIRMS portal used for FC-GPR. Register first. AIFs file by email in an Excel format provided by the RBI. For how FC-GPR, FC-TRS and the FLA return differ, read FC-GPR vs FC-TRS vs FLA Return: Which RBI Form Applies to You?

What if you miss it

Missing the FLA return is a FEMA contravention. The usual route is the late submission fee, available for up to three years from the due date. For the FLA return the fee is a flat ₹7,500 per return. If you have missed it, speak to us before you apply, because the route depends on how late the return is.
