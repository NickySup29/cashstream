In short

Buyer deducted TDS but it is missing in Form 26AS or AIS? See why it happens to NRI sellers, what to check first, and how to get the credit before you file.

If TDS on your property sale is not showing in 26AS, the buyer may not have deposited it, may have deposited it wrongly, or may not have reported it against your PAN. Check Form 26AS (now Form 168) and AIS first, then ask the buyer for the certificate and challan details. Once the buyer files the statement, the credit should follow.

Where should you look first?

The call usually starts the same way. The buyer has paid, the money in the bank is less than the sale price, and the seller opens the tax statement to find the TDS not showing in 26AS. TDS on property sale not showing in 26AS is almost never a problem with the sale itself. The gap is somewhere in the buyer's paperwork, and it can be traced. Look at three places, in this order.

WhereWhat it showsWhat to look for
Form 26AS (Form 168 under the Income-tax Rules 2026)The tax deducted against your PAN, as reported by the buyerThe buyer's PAN or TAN, the amount, the date, and whether it sits under your PAN
Annual Information Statement (AIS)The same deduction, with other information the Department holds on youThe sale value and the TDS, and whether the two agree
TDS certificate from the buyer (Form 131, earlier Form 16A; Form 132 on the PAN route described below)What the buyer says was deducted and depositedThe buyer's TAN or PAN, your PAN, and the challan details

The first two tell you whether the Department has the deduction. The third tells you what the buyer says was done. If the certificate shows a deduction but the statements do not, the gap is on the buyer's side, so ask for the certificate before anything else. For the rules on the deduction itself, read TDS on Sale of Property by NRI.

Why is the TDS deducted but not showing in Form 26AS?

When a resident buys property from an NRI, the buyer must deduct tax on the sale price under Section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act 2025 (earlier Section 195), deposit it, and report it against the seller's PAN. Only that report makes the credit appear. When TDS is deducted but not in 26AS for an NRI seller, the cause falls into one of four groups.

  • •

    No deposit. The buyer deducted the tax, or paid you the net amount, but never paid it to the Government.

  • •

    Short deposit. The buyer deposited less than was deducted.

  • •

    Wrong deposit. A wrong PAN, challan or period was used, so the tax sits against someone else.

  • •

    Not reported. The tax was paid, but the buyer's statement was late, wrong or never filed.

If TDS is not reflecting in 26AS, waiting for it to correct itself rarely works. If the buyer's report has been filed correctly, the credit should show once the statement is processed. If it does not, we chase the buyer straight away.

Credit is there, but the numbers do not match

A TDS mismatch in 26AS is a different problem from a missing credit. The credit shows, but the amount, date or PAN differs from your sale deed or the buyer's certificate. Common causes are a co-owner's share booked under one PAN, a payment made in instalments and reported unevenly, or a typing error in the PAN. The fix is the same: the buyer corrects the statement, and you wait for the corrected credit before you file.

Why is TDS not showing in AIS when the bank shows a deduction?

Your bank credit shows the amount after deduction. AIS and Form 26AS show TDS only once the buyer's deduction has been reported to the Department. If the buyer deposited late, quoted a wrong PAN or never filed the statement, TDS is not showing in AIS even though your bank paperwork shows the money arrived net. The buyer's proof of deposit and filing is what closes the gap.

What changed from 1 October 2026?

A new route removes the need for a TAN in one case. From 1 October 2026, a resident individual or HUF buying property from a non-resident can deduct and deposit the tax using their PAN and report it in Form 141, Schedule E. The buyer then issues the certificate in Form 132. This follows CBDT Notification 121/2026, dated 22 September 2026.

Companies, firms and other buyers still need a TAN, and the earlier route continues for payments made before 1 October 2026. For you as the seller, two things follow. The buyer no longer has to wait for a TAN before filing, so we ask clients to request the certificate soon after the sale, instead of waiting for a quarter to end. And a buyer who says the TAN is still pending has less of an excuse.

What should you ask the buyer for?

The TDS certificate comes first, because one document carries the PAN, the amount and the challan details. Check four things on it.

  1. 1

    Your PAN is correct, letter by letter.

  2. 2

    The amount matches what was deducted from the price.

  3. 3

    The date of deposit is right.

  4. 4

    The tax was deposited against the right period.

If any item is wrong, the buyer must correct it. A wrong PAN, a short deposit and no deposit all need action from the buyer, not from you. Send a short email naming the item that is wrong, with the page of the certificate attached. Keep the sale agreement, the proof of the net payment you received and any reply from the buyer in one folder.

How long should you wait?

Not long. Once the buyer's statement is filed and processed, the credit should show. If it has not, chase the buyer immediately rather than at the end of the quarter. For how long a refund takes once the credit is in place, read How Long Does an NRI TDS Refund Take?

What if the credit is still missing when you file?

File the return and claim the credit. In our practice we first chase the buyer to pay the tax to the Government. If the buyer does not, we still claim the credit against the buyer's deduction, supported by the sale agreement, proof of deduction and the certificate.

The risk is a demand notice, because the system may process the return without the credit. When that happens, we ask the processing centre to look at it again with the evidence. If the credit is still refused, the matter goes in appeal. No outcome can be promised, because the result depends on the evidence and on the authority hearing the case. For the claim route, read How to Claim Back TDS on Property Sale.

What if the buyer will not fix it?

Go in this order. Each step is an escalation of the one before.

  1. 1

    The buyer. Ask in writing for the deposit or the correction, and keep the reply.

  2. 2

    The processing centre. When you file the return, claim the credit even though it is not showing, and ask for rectification if it is refused.

  3. 3

    The Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), if a demand follows. Appear with your evidence.

  4. 4

    The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal. Appeals beyond it go to the High Court, and only on a question of law.

If your refund is already stuck for another reason, read NRI TDS Refund Stuck or Failed.
