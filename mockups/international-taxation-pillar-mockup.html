What Is International Taxation
What this covers

One umbrella, six areas of practice

International taxation covers every tax matter touching more than one country, India dealing with foreign entities, or foreign entities dealing with India. The goal is always the same: make sure income isn't taxed twice, compliance is met in every jurisdiction involved, and every treaty or legal benefit available is actually claimed, not left on the table.

Common pitfalls

Mistakes we see most often

Treating It As an Afterthought

Cross-border tax planned after the transaction, instead of before it, when far fewer options remain.

Missing Procedural Requirements

Being technically eligible for a benefit but missing the TRC, Form 41, or Form 145/146 that's needed to actually claim it.

DIY Treaty Interpretation

Small differences in treaty article or fact pattern change the outcome entirely, this is rarely a safe area to self-serve.

No Documentation Ready

Cross-border filings get more scrutiny, not less. Documentation gathered after a notice arrives is a much harder position.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is international taxation?+
International taxation covers every tax matter that touches more than one country, a foreign company earning income in India, an NRI with Indian investments, or an Indian business paying a vendor abroad. The goal is always the same: making sure income isn't taxed twice, and that every treaty benefit available is actually claimed.
What is a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA)?+
A DTAA is a tax treaty between India and another country that decides which country has the right to tax a specific type of income, and at what rate, so the same income isn't taxed twice. India has DTAAs with more than 90 countries.
How many countries does India have a tax treaty with?+
India has Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements with more than 90 countries, including the United States, United Kingdom, UAE, Singapore, and Germany. Each treaty sets its own rates for dividends, interest, royalties, and fees for technical services.
Do NRIs have to pay tax in India?+
Yes, but only on income earned or received in India, rental income, interest, capital gains, or dividends from Indian sources. Income earned entirely outside India is generally not taxable for a Non-Resident, unlike for a full Resident, whose worldwide income is taxed in India.
What's the difference between NRI, RNOR, and Resident status for tax purposes?+
Your status decides how much of your income India can tax.
StatusWhat Gets Taxed in India
Non-Resident (NRI)Only income earned or received in India
RNORIndian income, plus foreign income from a business controlled in India
Resident & Ordinarily ResidentWorldwide income
What is withholding tax (TDS) on payments to non-residents?+
Withholding tax, called TDS in India, is tax the payer deducts before paying a non-resident, at a rate set by the Income Tax Act or the applicable treaty, whichever is more beneficial. It applies to payments like royalty, interest, dividends, and fees for technical services.
How can I avoid double taxation on income earned in more than one country?+
Through the DTAA between India and the other country, which lets you claim an exemption, a lower treaty rate, or a Foreign Tax Credit for tax already paid abroad. The right method depends on the treaty and the type of income, and needs the correct documentation, usually a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 41, filed before or with your return.
Do I need a Chartered Accountant for international tax matters?+
It's strongly recommended. Treaty interpretation is fact-specific, small differences in the nature of income, the treaty article, or the documentation on file change the outcome entirely, and DIY approaches are one of the most common reasons claims get rejected or delayed.
