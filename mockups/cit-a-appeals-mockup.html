What this service covers

The first appeal, and often the most important one

When you disagree with an assessment order, the first level of appeal is before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), or the Joint Commissioner (Appeals) for smaller-value cases. This isn't a formality, it's the stage where most disputes actually get resolved, or where the record gets built for everything that follows if they don't.

We draft the grounds of appeal, the statement of facts, and a detailed written submission with case law support, then represent you through what is now a largely faceless appellate process. The goal is straightforward: get the addition, disallowance, or penalty reversed or reduced here, rather than carrying it forward.

A well-drafted appeal at this stage often avoids years of further litigation. Under-preparing here is the most expensive mistake in the entire appeal process, not the cheapest one.
Income Tax Act, 2025 update

Which Act actually applies to your appeal

The same principle that governs scrutiny notices applies to appeals: Section 536 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 keeps the old Income-tax Act, 1961 governing any proceeding relating to a tax year that began before 1 April 2026. The Department's own published guidance confirms appeals against orders for earlier years continue under the old Act.

Since you can only appeal an order that already exists for a past year, your appeal is filed under Section 246A, using Form 35, under the old Act, not the new one.

The question everyone asks first

Do you have to pay before you appeal?

This is the question almost everyone asks first, and the honest answer has three parts.

1

There's no automatic stay

Filing an appeal doesn't, by itself, pause recovery of the disputed demand. A specific stay application has to be filed alongside the appeal, or recovery can proceed while your case is still pending.

2

The standard requirement is a 20% pre-deposit

Under current CBDT practice, paying 20% of the disputed tax demand is generally sufficient to obtain a stay on the remaining 80% while the appeal is heard. The specific amount can sometimes be negotiated depending on the facts, but 20% is the number to plan around.

3

Not applying is the actual mistake, not the 20%

The clients who end up with real cash-flow problems during an appeal are almost always the ones who never filed a stay application at all, not the ones who paid the standard deposit.

Common pitfalls

Mistakes that create risk

Missing the Appeal Filing Deadline

Generally 30 days from receipt of the order, and this is the single most unforgiving deadline in the entire process.

Filing Vague, Boilerplate Grounds

Grounds without proper legal drafting reduce your chance of relief even if the underlying position is strong.

Not Applying for Stay of Demand

This is what leads to coercive recovery while your appeal is still pending, not the appeal itself.

Submitting Fresh Evidence Improperly

Additional evidence has its own route, and skipping it can get that evidence excluded entirely.

Treating CIT(A) as a Formality

This stage is often the best chance to resolve a dispute, not a box to check before the "real" appeal at ITAT.

What's at stake

Missing this stage has consequences that compound

  • The demand becomes final and recoverable if the appeal isn't filed in time
  • Bank accounts can be attached, and refunds adjusted against the disputed demand
  • Weak grounds of appeal reduce the chance of relief even later, at the ITAT stage
  • Interest keeps accruing on the disputed demand throughout the appeal process
  • Genuine claims get permanently lost if not raised with proper evidence at this stage

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the CIT(A) appeal process take?+
It varies with case complexity and the current backlog at the appellate authority, but faceless processing has generally made timelines more predictable than they used to be. We give a realistic estimate specific to your case once we've reviewed the order.
Do I have to pay the disputed tax before or during the appeal?+
Not the full amount. The standard practice is a 20% pre-deposit of the disputed demand to obtain a stay on the remaining 80% while your appeal is heard, but there's no automatic stay, you have to apply for it alongside the appeal.
Can I submit new documents that weren't given to the Assessing Officer?+
Generally yes, through the proper additional-evidence procedure, provided there's a valid reason the evidence wasn't produced earlier. Submitting it outside that procedure risks having it excluded.
What are my chances of winning?+
This depends entirely on the specific facts, the quality of the original documentation, and how the grounds of appeal are drafted, there's no honest general answer to this question. What we can tell you, after reviewing your actual order, is how strong the position looks and what it would take to argue it well.
What happens if CIT(A) also rules against me?+
You can appeal further to the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, generally within 60 days of the CIT(A) order. This is exactly why we draft the CIT(A) submission with that possibility already in mind.
What is Form 35, and do I need a CA to file it?+
Form 35 is the prescribed form for filing an appeal with CIT(A) or JCIT(A), including the statement of facts and grounds of appeal. You're not legally required to use a CA to file it, but the grounds of appeal are legal drafting, not a form-filling exercise, and weak grounds are difficult to fix later.
Can I appeal a penalty order separately from the assessment order?+
Yes. Penalty orders are separately appealable, and often need their own grounds distinct from the underlying assessment dispute.
Does a reassessment order qualify for a CIT(A) appeal too?+
Yes, reassessment orders are among the orders that can be appealed to CIT(A), the same 30-day filing window and stay-of-demand considerations apply.
How much does CIT(A) appeal representation cost?+
Fees depend on the complexity of the case and the volume of documentation involved, and are discussed transparently during your initial consultation before any work begins.
