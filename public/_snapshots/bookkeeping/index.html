The problem

Books that wait cost more to fix

Most businesses come to us when something has already happened: a notice from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), a request from an investor or lender, or a tax deadline that arrived before the books were ready. Four problems tend to follow.

1

An IRS review finds records you can't support

If the IRS examines a return, you need records behind every figure. Books built after the fact take longer to produce and leave more gaps.

2

Your tax returns carry errors

Returns prepared from uncategorized or unreconciled books can contain wrong figures, and correcting them later means reopening returns you've already filed.

3

Investors and lenders lose confidence

Due diligence starts with financial statements. Books that can't be reconciled slow down a raise, a loan or a sale.

4

Vendors and partners take you less seriously

Unclear balances and slow answers about what you owe, or what you're owed, change how other businesses deal with you.

The cause is usually the same: the books were left until the last date instead of being cleaned up every month.

Behind or unreliable

Catch up bookkeeping and bookkeeping cleanup

Many of the businesses we hear from are either behind on their books or have books they can't rely on. We handle both.

Catch up bookkeeping

You need catch-up bookkeeping when months have passed without regular bookkeeping: receipts piled up, statements unreconciled, transactions never entered. The work is to gather the records, enter them and reconcile your accounts until the books are current.

Bookkeeping cleanup

You need bookkeeping cleanup when books exist but can't be trusted: transactions in the wrong categories, a chart of accounts that no longer fits the business, accounts that don't match bank statements, or mixed-up records. The goal is books that match your statements and support your tax return.

Most jobs involve both, so we treat bookkeeping cleanup services and catch up bookkeeping services as one piece of work: find out what is missing, fix what is wrong, then hand over books you can keep current every month.

What audit-ready books look like

Audit-ready means that for every figure in your books and on your return, you can show the record behind it: transactions categorized, accounts reconciled to your statements, and supporting documents such as bills and receipts organized and easy to find.

The IRS says your books must show your gross income, deductions and credits, and that supporting documents back up the entries in your books and on your tax return.

Source: IRS, What kind of records should I keep?

How long to keep your records

The IRS sets out how long different records should be kept. In general terms:

How long to keep records, according to the IRS
SituationHow long to keep records
The general rule3 years from the date you filed your original return
You file a claim for credit or refund after filing3 years from filing, or 2 years from the date you paid the tax, whichever is later
You left out income that is more than 25% of the gross income shown on your return6 years
You claim a loss from worthless securities or a bad debt deduction7 years
Employment tax recordsAt least 4 years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later
You don't file a return, or file a fraudulent oneIndefinitely

Keep copies of your filed tax returns as well. This is general guidance, and your situation may differ.
