NRI Rental Income From India and DTAA: How to Avoid Paying Tax Twice

Reviewed by Cash Stream Advisors. Last reviewed: 23 November 2026.

The short answer

The treaty only decides the credit you get at home. A lower-deduction certificate, applied for before the year starts, can reduce the deduction.

A flat in Chennai, a landlord in New Jersey, and a tenant who has just been told to deduct tax before paying the rent. Most NRI landlords ask the same thing at this point: surely the tax treaty fixes this? It does not, and knowing why saves a lot of confusion. This guide covers NRI rental income tax in India, and what the DTAA really does for it.

The short answer

India taxes rent from Indian property whichever country the owner lives in. The tenant must deduct TDS at the rate in force for a non-resident, and no treaty lowers that rate. The treaty only decides the credit you get at home. A lower-deduction certificate, applied for before the year starts, can reduce the deduction. For the papers a treaty claim needs on other income, read Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) for DTAA: Who Issues It and What India Needs and Form 10F (Now Form 41): When You Need It and How to File It Online.

Who this affects

  • An NRI landlord in the US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Canada or Australia with a house or flat let out in India.

  • A family or tenant in India paying rent to an NRI, who must deduct the tax.

  • A returning NRI who still has rent from India and is moving between tax statuses.

What the tenant deducts and why

The tenant must deduct tax on rent paid to an NRI under Section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act 2025 (old Section 195), from the first rupee, with no monthly limit. The rate is the rate in force for a non-resident individual: 30% plus surcharge, where it applies, and 4% cess. That is 31.2% before any surcharge. Because a tenant often does not know the landlord's total income, many tenants and banks deduct at the maximum rate with surcharge, which can go to around 38% or more, and the landlord claims the difference back. If the landlord has no PAN, a higher rate can apply, so give the tenant your PAN.

This is the point on which NRI rental income tax usually surprises people: the deduction is on gross rent, but your tax is on net rent. You can deduct 30% of the rent as a standard deduction (old Section 24(a)) and municipal taxes paid, and a loan interest deduction in some cases. The gap is claimed back as a refund in your return. For the refund timeline, see our NRI TDS refund guides.

Illustration (hypothetical, round numbers): rent of ₹1 lakh a month is ₹12 lakh a year. At 31.2%, the tenant deducts about ₹3.74 lakh. After the 30% deduction, the taxable rent is ₹8.4 lakh, and the tax due on it can be much lower than the TDS. The difference comes back as a refund when the return is filed.

The tenant's steps:

  1. Get a TAN, which is needed for deducting tax on an NRI payment.

  2. Deduct and deposit the tax on each payment.

  3. File the quarterly statement of deduction for payments to non-residents, which is Form 144 under the Income-tax Rules 2026 (old Form 27Q).

  4. Give the landlord the TDS certificate.

Readers also search for this as TDS on rent paid to NRI, TDS on rent to NRI, tax on rental income for NRI or NRI rental income TDS. The answer is the same: Section 393(2) applies from the first rupee, and the tenant, not the landlord, is responsible for deducting.

Does the DTAA reduce the TDS on rent?

No. The property article (Article 6 in the US, UK, UAE, Singapore and Australia treaties) gives the taxing right to the country where the property is situated, with no cap on its rate. India can therefore tax the rent in full, and a residence certificate and Form 41 do not lower the deduction.

What the treaty changes is the second step. Your home country may also tax the rent, but it gives credit for the Indian tax. That credit is what avoids double tax. For how the credit works, read Foreign Tax Credit in India: Form 67, Form 44 and US Form 1116 Explained. The country pages cover the US and the UK: India-USA DTAA: Treaty Rates, Forms and How to Claim Them and India-UK DTAA: Treaty Rates, Pensions and How to Claim Them.

The same question comes up as NRI rental income DTAA: people expect a treaty rate on rent as there is on dividends and interest. The answer for rent is no rate cap, only credit at home.

Lower-deduction certificate (Form 128)

A lower-deduction certificate lets the tenant deduct at a lower rate, or nil, because your actual tax is lower than the standard deduction rate. It is applied for under Section 395 on Form 128, on the TRACES portal, with a computation of your estimated income and tax. A PAN is required. The Department says an application cannot be processed once the transaction is complete, so apply before the rent is paid. In our practice we apply about three months before the year starts, so the certificate is ready for the first rent.

Where claims go wrong

  1. No Indian return filed. Fix: file, to claim the refund and to support credit abroad.

  2. No TDS certificate. Fix: ask the tenant for it each quarter.

  3. Treating the residence certificate as a once-only paper. Fix: renew it each year.

  4. Tenant deducting at the wrong rate. Fix: give the tenant a written rate, or a lower-deduction certificate, before the first payment.

  5. No credit claimed at home. Fix: use the foreign tax credit form for the year.

Illustration (hypothetical, not a client matter): an NRI landlord lets a flat to a family. The tenant deducts the full rate and the landlord never files a return, because "TDS is already paid". He loses the refund of the excess, and his home-country credit is hard to support without the Indian return.

A short note for tenants

A tenant is the person who must deduct. If you pay rent to an NRI, agree the rate with the landlord in writing, hold a TAN and keep the certificate.

Questions people ask about NRI rental income and DTAA

Do I pay tax twice on my Indian rental income?

Not if you claim credit. India taxes the rent first, because the property is here. Your home country then taxes the same rent but gives credit for the Indian tax, usually capped at its own tax on that income. Some countries, such as the UAE, have no personal income tax, so the Indian tax is the only one.

Does the DTAA reduce the TDS my tenant deducts?

No. Under the property article of each treaty, the country where the property is situated may tax the rent, and the treaty sets no lower rate. A residence certificate and Form 41 do not change that. The treaty matters for the credit in your home country, not for the rate the tenant deducts.

Do I need a tax residency certificate to claim DTAA benefits on rent?

Not to reduce the TDS, because the treaty gives no reduction on rent. You need it where you rely on the treaty for another income, or to support credit abroad. Keep it for the year anyway, with Form 41, since the same papers help for interest and dividends.

What is a lower-deduction certificate and when do I apply?

It is a certificate from the Income Tax Department under Section 395, applied for on Form 128, that lets the tenant deduct at a lower rate or nil. Apply before the tax year starts, so it is ready for the first rent. In our practice that means applying about three months ahead.

Do I need to file an Indian return if TDS was deducted?

Yes, if you want a refund of excess TDS, and generally in any case when your income is above the filing limit. The return shows the rent, the 30% deduction and the tax actually due. Without it, the tenant's deduction stays with the department and you cannot claim credit abroad.

Which country lets me avoid home tax on Indian rental income?

Few do entirely. The UAE has no personal income tax, and Singapore does not tax most foreign income received by individuals. The US, UK, Canada and Australia tax worldwide income but give credit or an offset for Indian tax. The result depends on your residence, so check before you file.

What does my tenant have to do?

The tenant must hold a TAN, deduct tax at the right rate on each payment, deposit it with the government, file the quarterly statement and give you the TDS certificate. A tenant who deducts less than required can be treated as in default, so it helps to give the tenant a written rate in advance.
