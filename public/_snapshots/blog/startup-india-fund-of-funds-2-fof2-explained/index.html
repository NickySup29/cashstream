Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0: Rs. 10,000 Crore and What It Actually

April 26, 2026

The government just committed Rs. 10,000 crore to back Indian startups. Not directly, but through a structure that most founders have never heard of and most CAs have never explained properly. Let's fix that.

The Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 (FoF 2.0) was officially notified by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) on April 25, 2026 via a letter to SIDBI. The implementation agency is SIDBI, the same bank that ran FoF 1.0. The scheme is live with immediate effect.

What Is a Fund of Funds and Why Does It Matter?

A Fund of Funds does not write cheques to startups directly. It puts money into Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) registered with SEBI, and those AIFs then invest into startups. Think of it as the government becoming a limited partner in VC funds, which then become investors in your startup.

This structure exists for a reason. DPIIT and SIDBI are not equipped to evaluate thousands of individual startups. But they can evaluate 30 to 50 professional VC fund managers and let those managers do the ground-level work. The government provides the capital base, and the private sector provides the judgment.

The Four Buckets of Investment

FoF 2.0 has divided its deployment into four segments, and the terms differ significantly across each one.

The first segment is deep tech startups. AIFs in this bucket can have any corpus size, can run up to 18 years, and can receive up to 40% of their corpus from FoF 2.0 subject to a ceiling of Rs. 500 crore per AIF. The investment multiplier requirement is 1.5x, meaning for every rupee committed by FoF 2.0, the AIF must invest Rs. 1.50 into DPIIT-recognized deep tech startups. To qualify, the AIF's private placement memorandum must explicitly reference support for deep tech startups as defined under DPIIT's Gazette Notification GSR 108(E) dated February 4, 2026.

The second segment is smaller AIFs supporting early-stage startups, essentially Micro VCs with a corpus capped at Rs. 400 crore. These AIFs can receive up to 30% of their corpus from FoF 2.0 subject to a ceiling of Rs. 100 crore. Their life is capped at 10 years and they must commit at least 50% of their corpus to seed and early-stage deals, with per-startup investment limited to Rs. 10 crore. The investment multiplier here is 2x.

The third segment covers tech-driven and innovative manufacturing startups. These AIFs can also run up to 18 years and receive up to 30% of corpus from FoF 2.0 subject to a ceiling of Rs. 200 crore. The multiplier is 1.75x and the AIF must focus on at least one of the government-defined champion sectors for manufacturing.

The fourth and final segment is sector or stage agnostic AIFs. No additional eligibility requirements, corpus cap up to Rs. 180 crore from FoF 2.0, life up to 12 years, and the highest multiplier of the lot at 2.5x.

The Selection Process Is Two-Stage

AIFs don't just apply and get funded. First, a Venture Capital Investment Committee (VCIC) screens proposals. The VCIC includes external ecosystem experts appointed by DPIIT and representatives from SIDBI. AIFs make a detailed presentation and the VCIC recommends or rejects.

If the VCIC recommends, it goes to a sub-committee of SIDBI's Board for sanction. Only after Board-level sanction does a Letter of Intent get issued, followed by a Contribution Agreement.

Importantly, the total government contribution to any single AIF from all Ministries and State Governments combined cannot exceed 50% of that AIF's corpus. The VCIC can set a stricter limit based on market conditions.

The 50% Cap Has a Real Implication for VC Funds

If you are a VC fund manager reading this, pay attention to this point. FoF 2.0 money can cover at most half your corpus. You still need to raise the rest from private LPs. This is intentional. The government wants to co-invest alongside private capital, not substitute it.

This also means FoF 2.0 participation is a signal of quality for your fund, not a free pass to go government-funded. The more credible your LP base, the better positioned you are in the VCIC evaluation.

What Happens to Returns?

Returns from FoF 2.0, after deducting up to 5% for capacity building activities like workshops, mentorship and plug-and-play facilities, go back to the Consolidated Fund of India. This is taxpayer money at work and the government wants it returned with interest.

SIDBI's operational costs are capped at 0.50% per annum of total AIF commitments outstanding. Any temporarily idle funds with SIDBI earn interest at the prevailing RBI repo rate, credited annually on March 31.

What This Means for Startups

If you are a DPIIT-recognized startup, you don't interact with FoF 2.0 directly. You pitch to AIFs that are backed by FoF 2.0. So the practical impact for founders is an expansion in the number of active VC funds in India, particularly in deep tech, early-stage, and manufacturing-linked sectors that have historically been underfunded.

The scheme also specifically asks SIDBI to give weight to startups beyond metro regions when selecting AIFs. If you are building something in Tier 2 or Tier 3 India, this is the most meaningful structural support the government has offered in years.

What This Means for Tax and Compliance Advisors

For CA and CS professionals, FoF 2.0 activity will generate a new wave of AIF formations, SEBI registrations, contribution agreement reviews, fund structuring work, and startup due diligence mandates. The multiplier requirements mean AIFs will be under pressure to deploy fast and into DPIIT-recognized entities. That recognition status will become even more valuable, and the compliance pipeline around it will grow.

Bottom Line

Rs. 10,000 crore is serious capital. The structure is thoughtful, the multiplier requirements keep private capital in the game, and the focus on deep tech and manufacturing shows the government is thinking beyond app-based consumer startups this time. Whether the implementation matches the intent depends entirely on how SIDBI and the VCIC run the selection process.

Watch that space closely.
