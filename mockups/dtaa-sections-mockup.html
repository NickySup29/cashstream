Quick answer

What is DTAA?

DTAA stands for Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. It is a treaty between India and another country that stops the same income from being taxed in full in both places. A DTAA does not remove tax. It decides which country may tax the income, caps the rate where it allows tax at source, and gives relief in the other country, either as an exemption or as a credit for the tax already paid.

In one line

A treaty decides who taxes first and gives relief in the other country.

How relief works

Two ways a treaty gives relief

Most treaties use one of two methods. Which one applies depends on the treaty article and on your country of residence.

1

The exemption method

The income is taxed in only one country, and the other country leaves it out of its tax. Which country keeps the right to tax depends on the article of the treaty.

2

The credit method

Both countries may tax the income. Your country of residence then gives you credit for the tax already paid in the source country, so you do not pay the same tax twice. For an Indian resident with foreign income, this credit is claimed with Form 67 (Section 90 or 90A, now Section 159, or Section 91, now Section 160).

Does a DTAA reduce TDS on rental income from India?

No. Rental income from property in India is taxed in India under the immovable property article of the treaty (Article 6), so the treaty does not lower the TDS your tenant deducts. What lowers TDS at source is a Lower Deduction Certificate under Section 197 (now 395(1)), applied for on Form 128. The treaty still helps in your home country: a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F support your claim for credit for the tax paid in India on your overseas return.

Foreign companies

Permanent establishment: the test that decides if India can tax a foreign company

A foreign company's business profits are taxable in India only if it has a permanent establishment here. Without one, the treaty usually protects those profits from Indian tax.

Permanent establishment
  • A fixed place of business in India, such as an office, branch or factory, can create a permanent establishment.
  • A person in India who regularly concludes contracts for the foreign company can create one, even without an office.
  • Some treaties also treat long-running service projects as a permanent establishment, so the treaty wording matters.

A liaison office is treated differently from a branch. See the question on liaison offices below.

Technical services

The "make available" clause

Several of India's treaties, including some with major partners, tax fees for technical services in India only when the service makes technical knowledge available to the person paying. In plain terms, the recipient must be able to use that knowledge on its own afterwards, without calling the provider again. A routine service that only delivers a result does not meet that test.

Documents

Documents you typically need

To claim treaty relief, the payer in India or the tax office will usually ask for proof of where you are resident and some supporting details. Having these ready is what keeps TDS at the treaty rate instead of the full domestic rate.

Tax Residency Certificate (TRC)

Issued by the tax authority of your country of residence. It is the main proof that you are resident there. For US residents the equivalent is the certificate from the IRS (Form 6166).

Form 10F (now Form 41)

A self-declaration with details that the TRC may not show, such as your status, tax identification number in your country and the period of residence. A filled Form 10F is usually given to the payer along with the TRC.

No permanent establishment declaration

For companies: a statement that the company has no permanent establishment in India.

PAN, or the details asked in its place

Needed for the Indian tax records and the TDS certificate.

Payment details

The agreement, invoice or payment advice showing the type and amount of income.

Form 67 and proof of tax paid in India

For the foreign tax credit claim on your overseas return.

Lower Deduction Certificate (Form 128)

Where TDS must be reduced at source, for example on property sale or rental income.
