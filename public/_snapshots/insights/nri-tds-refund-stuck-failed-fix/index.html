In short

NRI refund not credited? See the usual causes, from bank validation to demand adjustment, how to raise a refund reissue request, and when to get help.

NRI refunds usually stall for a few identifiable reasons. The most common are a bank account that is not validated or has gone inactive, an Aadhaar and PAN mismatch, unanswered queries, and older demands. Most can be fixed with a fresh refund reissue request, without refiling the return.

First, is the problem the refund or the credit?

If your NRI TDS refund has not been received for weeks, the first question decides everything: is the money missing from the Department's records, or is it there and not reaching you? If the TDS is not in your tax statement or AIS, the problem is the credit, not the refund. The buyer has not filed the TDS return, or has filed it wrongly, and there is nothing for the Department to refund yet. Fix that first by following up with the buyer. Our post on TDS Not in Form 26AS or AIS covers it.

If the credit is there and the refund is not reaching you, the problem is the refund. A credit lower than expected is a third case. For example, you may expect a credit of ₹10 lakh and see only ₹2 lakh. That too needs a follow up with the buyer, because the Department can refund only what it has on record.

Why is my income tax refund not received?

Most cases of an income tax refund not received that we see come back to a handful of causes: a bank account that is not validated or has gone inactive, an Aadhaar and PAN mismatch, an unanswered query, or an older demand. An ITR refund not received looks the same from outside whichever the cause is, so match what the portal shows to the table below.

NRI refund bank account validation: which account works?

Refunds are paid only to a pre-validated Indian bank account linked to your PAN. In our files, an active NRO account, validated on the e-filing portal, is what works. Ordinary savings accounts generally fail, and a foreign account may not work either. NRI refund bank account validation is the single step that decides the most cases.

The common mistake is an account that has gone stale. Many NRIs leave the account unused, and its KYC lapses. The account must be operative, and the KYC must be kept current, for the refund to land. Fix the account before you file, not after the refund fails.

Also check the name. The name on the account should match the name on your PAN, because a mismatch can quietly cause a failure.

How do you raise a refund reissue request?

When the portal shows the refund as failed, you raise a refund reissue request. It is not a new return. In our experience it is a seamless step once the cause is fixed, so a failed income tax refund notice for an NRI is rarely the end of the road. The request can be raised once the processing centre has told you the refund failed.

  1. 1

    Fix the cause first: the account, the query, or the Aadhaar and PAN link.

  2. 2

    Log in to the e-filing portal, open the Services menu and choose Refund reissue.

  3. 3

    Create a new request and select the year in which the refund failed.

  4. 4

    Check the bank account shown, validate it if it is not validated, select it and submit.

  5. 5

    Track the status and keep the confirmation.

We do not wait. Before raising the request, we check with the refund banker and the processing centre, and then the portal, so the request goes in once and goes in right. Delay costs you the use of the money, although interest runs on a delayed refund. Under Section 437 of the Income-tax Act 2025 (earlier Section 244A), the Department pays simple interest at 0.5% for each month or part of a month, but no interest is paid where the refund is less than 10% of the tax determined. For how long refunds take when nothing goes wrong, read How Long Does an NRI TDS Refund Take?

What about Aadhaar and PAN?

If your PAN has become inoperative because it is not linked to Aadhaar, the refund can be held. This is a big issue in our files. You approach your jurisdictional Assessing Officer to link them. If you hold an Aadhaar, it is a fairly straightforward process, and in some of our cases it has taken only a couple of days.

Not every NRI is affected. NRIs who meet the non-resident criteria under the tax law, and who are not required to hold an Aadhaar, are generally exempt from linking. Living abroad is not enough by itself, so keep proof of non-resident status ready, such as the passport with travel history, and tell the Assessing Officer if your PAN has been marked inoperative.

What if the refund was adjusted against a demand?

An earlier-year demand is a big cause of stuck refunds. The Department can set the refund off against that demand. Look for it in the intimation and in the outstanding demand section of the portal.

Then approach the Assessing Officer. The officer is the authority who decides on the adjustment, so we meet the officer and discuss the next step. We do not promise the outcome.

What about unanswered queries?

The processing centre may raise a query. Many NRIs do not see it or do not reply, and then the refund does not move. Check the portal for pending queries, and reply to each one with the papers asked for.

When is it worth getting help?

Get help early, before the return is filed, not after the refund fails. We ask clients to come at the start so the account, the Aadhaar and PAN link, the TDS credit and any old demand are sorted before filing. In our files this keeps failed attempts to a minimum. It does not guarantee a result.
