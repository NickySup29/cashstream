In short

Buyer deducted TDS on your Indian property sale? See who can claim it back, the ITR route, the documents, and why 26QB refund advice does not fit NRI sellers.

An NRI seller claims back excess TDS by filing an Indian income tax return for the year of sale, once the buyer has deposited the tax and reported it in a TDS return. The credit then shows in your tax records, and the excess is refunded after processing. It is not a 26QB correction, which applies to resident sellers.

Which route applies to you?

The route depends on who you are and which form the buyer used. Most refund advice online is written for resident sellers, where the buyer deducts 1% and pays through Form 26QB. An NRI seller is on a different track: the buyer deducts at the full non-resident rate and reports it in Form 27Q (the quarterly TDS return for payments to non-residents, renumbered under the 2026 Rules).

If you are asking how to claim refund of 26QB, can NRI claim TDS refund, or can seller claim TDS refund on property sale, the answer is the same: the seller's money comes back only through the seller's own return. The buyer can claim back only tax paid in error. For the TDS rules themselves, read TDS on Sale of Property by NRI.

Why the TDS is often more than your tax

The buyer deducts TDS on the full sale price, but your tax is only on the gain. With a small gain or a loss, the excess TDS deducted on property sale can be several times your real tax.

Illustration. For a sale on or after 23 July 2024, the buyer of a long term property deducts about 14.95% of the price (12.5% plus 15% surcharge and 4% cess on a sale above ₹1 crore). On a ₹7 crore sale that is about ₹1.05 crore. If your gain is ₹1 crore, your tax is about ₹15 lakh, and about ₹90 lakh sits with the Department until your refund. If you made a loss, all of it is excess.

Check your credit before you claim

A refund can only come from tax that the Department can see against your PAN. Check these before you file.

  1. 1

    The buyer had a TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) and deposited the TDS.

  2. 2

    The buyer filed the quarterly TDS return for non-residents, Form 27Q, for the quarter of payment.

  3. 3

    The credit shows in your Form 26AS (Form 168 under the 2026 Rules) and your Annual Information Statement (AIS), the Department's records of tax credited to you.

  4. 4

    The PAN is yours, not a co-owner's or a typo.

  5. 5

    The amount and date match the sale deed and the payment.

  6. 6

    For joint owners, each owner's share of TDS shows under their own PAN.

  7. 7

    The buyer has given you the TDS certificate, Form 16A (Form 131 under the 2026 Rules), downloaded from TRACES.

If any line fails, fix it with the buyer before you file. For missing credits, read TDS Not in 26AS or AIS.

How to claim it through your return

Here is how to claim back TDS on property sale as an NRI, and how to claim TDS on sale of property in practice. You claim the excess by filing your return for the tax year of sale and showing the TDS as a credit. This is the order we follow for an NRI TDS refund on property sale.

  1. 1

    Compute the gain. Sale value less cost of acquisition, cost of improvement and sale expenses. For a sale on or after 23 July 2024, an NRI's long term gain is taxed at 12.5% without indexation. Claim any exemption, such as reinvestment in a house, only with proof.

  2. 2

    Pick the return. We file ITR-2 for an NRI with capital gains and no business income. If the NRI also has business or professional income in India, it becomes ITR-3.

  3. 3

    Report the sale. Fill the capital gains schedule with the dates, sale value, stamp duty value and cost figures, and mark your residential status as non-resident.

  4. 4

    Claim the credit. The TDS from Form 26AS comes into the TDS schedule. Check that it matches, then add your other Indian income and the tax on it.

  5. 5

    Choose the refund account. Add a pre-validated Indian bank account, usually your NRO account, with the name matching your PAN.

  6. 6

    File and verify. File by the due date, usually 31 July for individuals who need no audit, and e-verify within 30 days.

For the refund of TDS on sale of property timeline, read How Long Does an NRI TDS Refund Take?

Documents to keep ready

  • •

    Sale deed, to show the sale value and date.

  • •

    Purchase deed, to show the cost and purchase date.

  • •

    Proof of improvement cost: builder receipts, bills and bank entries.

  • •

    Proof of sale expenses, such as brokerage.

  • •

    Form 16A from the buyer, and your Form 26AS and AIS.

  • •

    Stamp duty value of the property on the date of sale.

  • •

    Proof of any exemption claimed, such as a new house purchase.

  • •

    Passport and travel days, to support non-resident status.

  • •

    PAN, and the NRO bank account details for the refund.

  • •

    The lower deduction certificate, if one was used.

If the buyer got it wrong

If the buyer made a mistake, only the buyer can fix the TDS return. The NRI cannot correct it, but can chase it, and should not file until the credit is right.

Wrong PAN or amount: the buyer files a correction to the TDS return on TRACES. The credit then moves to your PAN once it is processed.

Rate too high: nothing for the buyer to fix. You claim the excess through your return.

Rate too low: the buyer is liable for the shortfall, with interest. You pay any tax still due through your return.

TDS not deposited, or no return filed: the credit will not show. Ask the buyer to deposit and file. If they do not, keep proof that tax was deducted, such as the sale deed and payment records, and speak to us.

Filed late: the credit appears once the buyer files. The buyer pays the late fee and interest, not you.

Getting it right the first time

The simplest refund is the one you never need. If you apply for a lower deduction certificate before the sale, the buyer deducts close to your real tax, and far less money sits with the Department. In a ₹7 crore case of ours, the certificate cut the rate to 5.5% in 37 days. Apply at least two months before the sale date. For the method and a worked example, read Nil vs Lower TDS Certificate and Lower TDS Certificate for NRI Property Sale.
