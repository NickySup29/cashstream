In short

QuickBooks, Zoho Books or Xero? A plain comparison for small businesses: who each suits, what to check, and how to switch without a mess.

There is no single best. For a small US business, Zoho Books and QuickBooks are a close contest, with Xero just behind. QuickBooks is the name most US accountants know. Zoho Books is fast, integrates well and, in our experience, costs less. Pick the one your bookkeeper and your accountant can both run well, and check the vendor sites for current plans.

Every few weeks an owner asks us the same thing: which one should we use? QuickBooks vs Zoho Books vs Xero is the full question, and Zoho Books vs QuickBooks is the part we hear most. Xero vs Zoho Books is the other pairing people ask about. We work in all three, so this is what we see, and it is shorter than you might expect: choose the one your bookkeeper and your accountant can both run well.

One point on cost. Zoho's US pricing page lists a free plan for businesses below a revenue limit, with one user and one accountant. We have not quoted the limit, so check the vendor site before you rely on it.

When to choose each

QuickBooks Online suits a business whose accountant or CPA already uses it. US CPAs accept it most easily. The honest drawback is cost: in our experience it costs more than Zoho Books.

Zoho Books suits a cost-led business, or one that already uses other Zoho apps. It is fast and integrates well. Its drawback is familiarity: it is a growing choice among accountants, but fewer know it than QuickBooks, so ask your accountant first.

Xero follows just behind the other two in our experience. We run it for clients too. Tell us about your business and we will say whether it fits.

What we see in practice

QuickBooks is the easiest to hand over, because CPAs accept it most easily. The trouble spots are similar in every tool. Multi-currency needs weekly rate conversions, weekly analysis and invoice checks. Inventory is another, and we help with inventory costing. Receivable ledger balances, confirmation statements and bank feeds also need care, and we match them every month.

The rule we give clients is simple: choose what your bookkeeper and accountant can run. In one file we migrated the whole set of accounts in our structured format and let the client and the department heads try the new software before finishing the move. For help running a team's books, read What to Expect When You Outsource Your Bookkeeping.

Switching later

Yes, you can switch. The pain comes from a move that is unplanned, or from not having the agreement of the departments involved. With a clear plan, deadlines and milestones, and with accounting kept running in the old software until the migration is done, it can be managed. The part that matters most is managing expectations with the department heads.
