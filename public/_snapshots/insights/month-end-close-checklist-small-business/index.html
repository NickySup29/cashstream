In short

A month-end close checklist from a bookkeeping team's own monthly routine: what to do, in what order, and when the books are really closed.

A month-end close brings one month of books to a final state: bank accounts reconciled, receivables and vendor balances checked, taxes and payroll reconciled, reports reviewed, and the period locked. Our own close takes about five to six days. Below is our day-by-day routine, what people most often skip, and a free checklist that you can download and use for your own close.

Every month the work follows the same order: statements in, bank accounts first, receivables next, and down the list to the lock. Most closes go wrong in the steps that get skipped, not in the ones everybody knows. This is our month end close checklist, day by day, with the steps people skip most and a free spreadsheet you can use for your own close.

The month end close process, day by day

This is how we run a close. It takes about five to six days. Day 1 starts once we have your statements and invoices.

Day 4 holds the steps that are most often skipped, tax credits and vendor payments. A sixth day is sometimes needed, and what slows a close most is collecting the documents. For the reconciliations themselves, read Bank Reconciliation Not Matching? Common Errors and How to Fix Them.

What people skip

Four steps are skipped most often, and each one costs time later.

  • •

    Customer ledger confirmations. With your consent, we reach out to your customers as your finance team. We find differences between your ledger and theirs that nothing else shows.

  • •

    Vendor statements. We ask vendors for a ledger or confirmation statement, which helps us reconcile every account. Without it, vendor balances rest on one side's records.

  • •

    Business spending on personal cards and cash. Owners often fund the business from personal accounts and forget to send the statements. Expense booking, advances and prepaid expenses are then wrong.

  • •

    Tax credits. They are reconciled last and often skipped.

A skipped step does not go away. The difference turns up at the next close, or at year-end, when it is harder to trace.

What "closed" means

A month is closed when the period is locked, the reports are sent, and the open questions are cleared. We lock the period so that nobody can change it, after we have your consent and the consent of your accounts department, if you have one.

Each tool has its own feature. In QuickBooks Online, the closing date is set under Settings, Account and settings, then the Advanced tab, in the Accounting section, by turning on Close the books. You choose whether users see a warning, or a warning and a password, before they change anything dated on or before that date. In Zoho Books, the transaction lock sits under Accountant, then Transaction Locking, and you can lock one area at a time or everything at once. Zoho notes that transactions dated on the lock date itself can still be changed, so set the date to the day after your cut-off. Xero uses lock dates, set in its settings. For software choice, read QuickBooks vs Zoho Books vs Xero: Which Suits a Small Business.
