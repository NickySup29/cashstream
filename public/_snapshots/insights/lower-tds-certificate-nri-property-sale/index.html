In short

See how a lower TDS certificate rate is worked out for an NRI property sale, with a ₹7 crore case that got 5.5% against about 15% standard TDS.

A lower deduction certificate for NRI sellers works out the rate as the tax on your actual capital gain, expressed as a percentage of the sale value. In one of our cases, an NRI selling property for ₹7 crore received a 5.5% rate in 37 days, against about 15% the buyer would otherwise have deducted.

Why is TDS so much higher than the tax on the gain?

Because the buyer deducts on the full price, not on your gain. This is the core problem with TDS on sale of property by NRI above 1 crore: the buyer does not know your cost, so tax is cut from the gross sale consideration. On a sale of ₹5 crore, the deduction is worked on ₹5 crore.

The difference stays with the Department until you claim a refund. A lower deduction certificate can bring the deduction close to the real tax. For standard rates, read our guide to NRI property sale TDS.

How is the certificate rate worked out?

A lower tax deduction certificate for NRI sellers, also called a lower TDS deduction certificate for NRI sellers, rests on five steps.

  1. 1

    Compute the capital gain: sale value less cost of acquisition, cost of improvement and sale expenses. Cost is indexed only where the law allows it for the date of transfer. For an NRI transferring on or after 23 July 2024, it is not indexed.

  2. 2

    Compute the tax on that gain at the applicable rate, with surcharge and cess.

  3. 3

    Add the tax on your other Indian income for the year, and adjust for advance tax and TDS already credited.

  4. 4

    Express the total tax as a percentage of the sale value. That is the rate you ask for.

  5. 5

    The officer reviews the computation and the papers, then sets the final rate in the certificate.

The rate falls sharply when the gain is small because tax is charged only on the gain, but the rate is measured against the full sale value. Tax of about ₹38.5 lakh on a ₹7 crore sale is 5.5%. In our property cases on Insights, rates have run from 2.5% to 5.5%. For the nil and lower distinction, read Nil vs Lower TDS Certificate.

After 23 July 2024 there is no indexation: ₹7 crore less ₹2 crore less ₹2.425 crore leaves ₹2.575 crore, taxed at 14.95%. Before that date, indexed cost brings the gain down to about ₹74 lakh, taxed at 22.88% (20% plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess).

In both columns the buyer would deduct far more than the tax due. Improvement cost, and indexation where the date allows it, pull the rate down, which is why the cost papers matter.

What did the seller get wrong?

The seller had worked out the tax alone, and the computation had three errors. First, the cost treatment did not follow the rules for the date of transfer, so we recomputed the gain as the law requires. Second, the purchase year was wrong, and we corrected it from the purchase deed. Third, the gain itself was wrong, so we recomputed it with the improvement cost backed by builder receipts. The corrected figures supported the rate we applied for.

What did the officer ask?

The officer raised five queries over two rounds: the method used to compute income and capital gains, the purchase deed, the sale agreement, KYC of seller and buyer, and whether the purchase and sale were genuine. The capital gains query usually takes longest, as every cost figure must be matched to a paper. For typical timing, read How Long Does a Lower Deduction Certificate Take?

What moved the file?

Three things. Every reply came with its papers, so the officer never raised the same point twice. We followed up with the TDS Assessing Officer's office through several visits and calls, then with the Joint Commissioner's office once the internal report went for approval, then with TDS CPC on TRACES until issue. And no payment was made to the seller before the certificate arrived, which is why the full benefit was available.

How an NRI applies, in short steps

Here is how to apply for lower deduction certificate for NRI sellers, from abroad if needed.

  1. 1

    Who applies. The seller, as the certificate is for the person receiving the payment. An NRI can apply from abroad with a valid PAN, and a professional can prepare and track it.

  2. 2

    The form on TRACES. Register on TRACES under taxpayer login and fill Form 128 (Form 13 before 1 April 2026). Take every figure from your documents, as each must be supported before the officer.

  3. 3

    Documents. Sale agreement, purchase deed, improvement receipts, PAN and Aadhaar or passport of seller and buyer, the buyer's TAN where needed, the capital gains computation and a covering letter.

  4. 4

    Queries. Answer each in full, with papers. The officer then prepares a report for approval by the Joint or Additional Commissioner.

  5. 5

    Download. Once approved, download the certificate from TRACES and give a copy to the buyer before payment.

The application is verified electronically, and the option depends on whether the NRI holds Aadhaar, so we confirm it before filing. The NRI signs the covering letter and an authorisation for the professional. A registered power of attorney may be needed for the sale registration if the NRI cannot attend.

If TDS has already been deducted

A certificate applies only to payments made after it is issued, so it cannot undo TDS already deducted. Check the credit in Form 26AS or the Annual Information Statement, then claim the excess as a refund in your return. The refund follows processing, with interest where due. If part of the payment is still pending, a certificate can still help for that part.

The rate is the total tax on your gain, plus tax on other Indian income, less credits, expressed as a percentage of the sale value. The officer reviews your computation and papers, then sets the final rate in the certificate. In our ₹7 crore case the officer allowed 5.5%, including surcharge and cess.

At the standard 14.95% on ₹7 crore, the buyer would have deducted about ₹1.05 crore. At the certificate rate of 5.5%, the deduction was about ₹38.5 lakh, around ₹66 lakh less held back until a refund. No payment was made to the seller before the certificate arrived.
